1Business anatomy — Acquire / Engage / Retain / Monetize

  • Acquire — Experiences is itself an acquisition channel for the homes business (see §1). Its own demand comes from: (a) the redesigned Explore tab surfacing experiences to the ~500M+ existing Airbnb accounts; (b) local users (40% of Originals) — a net-new audience; (c) PR/brand halo (13,000 press stories, 660M social impressions on relaunch). S11 Supply comes from curated host applications (~60k) and event-driven supply (Olympics hosts). S2, S11
  • Engage — Core loop: browse photo-forward experiences on Explore → see who else is going → book (payment in chat) → attend → meet people → profile logs "connections made" → rebook. S3 The aha = a memorable in-person moment + a new social connection. Frequency is the strategic prize: experiences can be booked locally, weekly — breaking the twice-a-year travel ceiling. S2
  • Retain — Two switching costs unique to Experiences: (1) the social graph (people you met, can re-message) S3; (2) accumulated reviews + verified identity (200M+ verified IDs, 500M+ reviews across Airbnb). S2 Host retention: quality bar + curation keep the median high; event supply retained (>50% of Paris Games listings kept 6 months later). S1
  • Monetize — Single line: ~20% commission on each experience booking. S6 Expansion vectors: Delta miles rev-share (loyalty testbed) S1; Airbnb Originals (premium celebrity tickets) S3; eventual sponsored/promoted placement (in testing at parent level) S2. Not the near-term goal — "no meaningful revenue expected near term." S11

2Numbers that signal depth

Headline scale & product (Experiences-specific)

  • Relaunched globally May 13, 2025; Experiences live in ~650 cities; Services in ~260 cities. S3, S5
  • ~50,000+ experiences available post-relaunch. S12
  • Up to $200–250M invested in 2025 to scale Experiences + Services. S3, S11
  • ~60,000 host applications for the relaunch; 4.93/5 average experience rating. S11
  • Services launched with 10 categories (chefs, photography, massage, spa, personal training, catering, prepared meals, hair, makeup, nails). S3
  • Airbnb Originals: celebrity-hosted events (e.g., a day with Patrick Mahomes, Sabrina Carpenter). S3

Downstream conversion (the real KPI)

  • ~1 in 4 new experience guests go on to book a stay/service. S1
  • ~1 in 3 experience bookers book a stay within 90 days. S1
  • ~50% of experience bookings are from guests with no attached home booking. S2
  • 40% of Airbnb Originals bookings came from local users. S11

Market arcs

  • Local tours market ~$1.4T; foreign tours ~$320B — largest under-digitized travel category. S10
  • Experiences take rate ~20% vs. homes 15.5%; social-impact experiences 0%. S6

Parent context (why Experiences is a rounding error today)

  • ABNB Q1 2026 revenue $2.7B (+18% YoY); GBV $29B (+19%); guests spent ~$30B in Q1. S1
  • App nights +22% YoY = 63% of total nights (up from 58%). S1 First-time bookers +10% (highest since 2022). S1
  • CFO: "we don't expect meaningful revenue from our new businesses in the near term." S11 Blended into "Nights & Seats Booked" (+9% Q1) — Experiences is not separately disclosed. S1, S11

Audited financials (parent, FY2025 / latest Q)

  • FY2025 FCF $4.6B (38% margin); buybacks $3.8B (>80% of FCF); $11B corporate cash. S2
  • Q1 2026 adj. EBITDA $519M (+24%); FY2026 guide raised to low-to-mid-teens revenue growth, adj. EBITDA margin "≥35%." S1

Unit economics (Experiences, estimated)

  • Estimate: avg experience ticket ~$50–75 (vs. ~$150+ ADR × multi-night stay for homes) → per-booking revenue to Airbnb ≈ $10–15 at 20% take vs. ~$150–300 per home booking. Volume + frequency, not margin, is the lever. (Author estimate — Airbnb does not disclose experience ADR.)

3Wow Vault ★

Non-obvious layer — what makes an interviewer lean in. Ranked strongest first.

Experiences is an acquisition funnel dressed as a product line — its KPI is stays sold, not seats sold.
  • Mechanism: "~1 in 4 new guests who book an experience go on to book a stay or a service," and "about 1 in 3 people who book an experience book a stay within 90 days." S1 Management calls it "a great way to introduce new guests to everything Airbnb has to offer." S1
  • Why non-obvious: Everyone models Experiences as a low-take side business (it is — immaterial revenue). The real value is CAC: a cheap, high-frequency first touch that later converts into a high-AOV stay. Judge it by downstream home conversion, not its own GMV.
  • Deploy: "How would you measure a new marketplace category?" / "Should Airbnb keep Experiences given it loses money?" — recall hook: "the metric is the stay it seeds, not the seat it sells."
  • Source: S1
~50% of experience bookings come from guests with NO attached home booking.
  • Mechanism: "About half of experience bookings come from guests unattached to a homes booking — not staying with us in a home, may be staying in a hotel, may be not traveling at all." S2 40% of "Airbnb Originals" bookings came from local users S11 — people using Airbnb in their own city.
  • Why non-obvious: It quietly breaks the "big trip 1–2×/year" frequency ceiling that has always capped Airbnb. A local booking a Tuesday-night pottery class is a new use occasion.
  • Deploy: frequency/retention questions, "how does Airbnb grow beyond travel?" — recall hook: "Experiences turns a twice-a-year app into a weekend-plans app."
  • Source: S2, S11
The relaunch cut supply rather than adding it.
  • Mechanism: Airbnb paused new Experience host signups in April 2023, and in 2024 removed ~5,000 listings that under-booked or missed quality standards, then relaunched with a curated bar. S7, S8 The 2016 version failed partly because "most experiences offered are not unique and compete with traditional local tour operators." S8
  • Why non-obvious: Most PMs would fix a struggling marketplace by adding supply. Airbnb did the opposite — it cut the long tail to raise the median quality, because in an experiences marketplace a bad tour is a brand liability, not just a dead SKU.
  • Deploy: marketplace quality / cold-start / two-sided supply questions — recall hook: "they deleted supply to fix a supply business."
  • Source: S7, S8
City-by-city rollout ("go deep in one place to reach PMF") — Paris first for Experiences.
  • Mechanism: Rather than a global toggle, Experiences scales one city at a time — Paris first — "going deep in one place, reaching product-market fit," with "Airbnb Original experiences by local Parisians" showing "a really nice uptick." S2 This is Project Hawaii's "focus, shift, learn, scale" loop applied to a category. S2
  • Why non-obvious: Experiences liquidity is local, not global — a Paris tour needs Paris supply AND Paris (or Paris-bound) demand in the same 2-week window. National averages hide whether any single city actually has liquidity.
  • Deploy: marketplace launch strategy, geo-expansion, "how would you roll this out?" — recall hook: "experiences liquidity is measured per-city-per-week, not globally."
  • Source: S2
"Strong enough to stand alone, better together" — the Amazon-books analogy.
  • Mechanism: Chesky frames Experiences/Services the way Amazon added categories beyond books: each offering must survive on its own but compounds the whole. On Services, "once you solve one service, the next is only 20% different… then 10% different" — the marginal cost per new category falls. S1, S2
  • Why non-obvious: It reframes "category sprawl / distraction risk" into an efficiency flywheel. The bear case (Airbnb is unfocused) and the bull case (compounding platform) are the same fact seen two ways.
  • Deploy: strategy / focus / "is this a distraction?" questions — recall hook: "each next category is 20%, then 10% new work."
  • Source: S1, S2
Chatbots structurally can't sell experiences — 4 mechanism-level reasons.
  • Mechanism: (1) travel e-commerce is "photo-forward, not language-forward"; (2) "no direct manipulation — you can't touch anything"; (3) comparison at scale is impossible in chat; (4) "almost all bookings are multiplayer; chatbots are primarily single-player." Plus "not map-native." S1 Experiences are the most social and photo-forward of all — a group cooking class you book with friends.
  • Why non-obvious: Positions Experiences as the least AI-disintermediable inventory Airbnb has — exactly the visual, social, comparison-heavy shopping a text agent handles worst.
  • Deploy: "is AI a threat to Airbnb?" / AI-product questions — recall hook: "you don't buy a group pottery class in a chat window."
  • Source: S1
The social graph is the hidden second product inside Experiences.
  • Mechanism: The relaunch app lets you "see the other guests already going on an experience," "see the people you've already met," direct-message them, and shows a redesigned profile of "connections made along the way." S3 Later 2025 releases added direct messaging + social features. S3
  • Why non-obvious: Experiences is the only Airbnb surface where strangers meet in person by design. That produces a social graph a pure lodging OTA (Booking, Expedia) can't build — and social graphs create retention that room inventory can't.
  • Deploy: moat / retention / "what can Airbnb build that Booking can't?" — recall hook: "a hotel booking ends; an experience introduces you to people."
  • Source: S3
Delta miles on Experiences — solving the "no loyalty program" gap sideways.
  • Mechanism: The Delta partnership expanded so travelers "earn Delta miles on qualifying Airbnb Experiences and Services in addition to homes." S1 Airbnb is "the only large travel brand with no loyalty program" and says any program "wouldn't be an additive points program." S1
  • Why non-obvious: Experiences becomes a low-stakes testbed for loyalty mechanics (frequent, cheap, repeatable) before Airbnb commits to a homes loyalty program — you learn earning behavior on $50 tickets, not $2,000 stays.
  • Deploy: loyalty / monetization / partnership questions — recall hook: "they're prototyping loyalty on experiences, not on stays."
  • Source: S1
Experiences carries a 20% take rate — higher than the ~15.5% homes fee.
  • Mechanism: Airbnb charges hosts ~20% commission on Experiences vs. the 15.5% host-only fee that homes migrated to in late 2025. S6 Social-impact experiences take 0% (100% to nonprofit). S6
  • Why non-obvious: Per-booking economics are better than homes — the problem is never take rate, it's volume and average ticket size ($50 tour vs. $1,000 stay). So the growth lever is frequency, not monetization.
  • Deploy: monetization / unit-economics / "how do you make this profitable?" — recall hook: "20% of a $50 class is the constraint, not the take rate."
  • Source: S6
The market Airbnb is entering is huge but fragmented — local tours = ~$1.4T.
  • Mechanism: Tours-and-activities splits into foreign tours (~$320B) and local tours (~$1.4T). S10 It's the least-digitized major travel category — most experiences are still booked offline, cash, day-of.
  • Why non-obvious: The prize isn't taking share from Viator/GetYourGuide; it's moving offline demand online the way Airbnb did with spare rooms. Online penetration is single-digit — the TAM is mostly not-yet-online.
  • Deploy: TAM / market-sizing / "why enter this now?" — recall hook: "the competitor isn't Viator, it's cash and a paper flyer."
  • Source: S10

4Reframes & mental models to borrow

Airbnb's own framing devices, restated to use on any prompt.

  • "Strong enough to stand alone, but better together." Every new category must be independently viable AND compound the network. → strategy / portfolio / "should we build X?"
  • "Start bottom of the funnel." Airbnb's contrarian AI move — solve customer service (high stakes, can't hallucinate) before top-of-funnel discovery. Reads onto any AI feature: earn trust where errors are costly first. → AI product strategy.
  • "The trip, broadly defined." The unifying job isn't lodging — it's the whole trip (30-day stays, experiences, services). Experiences is one entry point into that job. → product vision / JTBD framing.
  • "Go deep in one place to reach PMF, then scale." (Project Hawaii: focus → shift → learn → scale.) City-by-city, not global-toggle. → launch strategy / geo-expansion.
  • "Each subsequent offering is 20%, then 10% new work." The marginal cost of adding a category falls each time. → "is this a distraction?" / platform economics.
  • "Events are the best way to add supply, not demand." (40k Paris Olympics hosts retained.) S2 Reads a demand spike as a way to acquire supply — applies to any experiences launch tied to a festival/game. → cold-start / supply questions.
  • "Ship the moment it's ready — giant moments aren't the way in the age of AI." The May release is a marketing showcase, not a feature gate. S2 → release strategy / cadence.

7Needs-based segments → problems → solutions

Shreyas method — cut on the JOB the user is hiring Experiences to do, never demographics. Each passes the 5-Point Test (consistent needs · product-specific · targetable · prioritizable · winnable).

Segmentation basis: the occasion + emotional job driving someone to book a paid activity — anchored on why now / who with / what feeling — because in experiences the same person is a different segment on a solo work trip vs. a group weekend vs. a local Tuesday night. Needs, not personas.

A. The "make this trip memorable" traveler — Job (functional): fill a free afternoon in a new city with something better than a generic tour. (Emotional): come home with a story, not just photos of a building. Friction: doesn't know what's good, fears the tourist-trap tour. Nudge (intrinsic): the aha of a genuinely local moment. Aha: "I did a thing only a local could have shown me." Today → gap: Airbnb surfaces experiences beside the stay, but discovery is weak and intent low → Play #1 (trip-attached smart itinerary).

B. The "we need a plan for the group" organizer — Job (functional): coordinate one activity 4 friends will all enjoy and pay for. (Social): be the person who found the great thing. Friction: multiplayer booking is painful; splitting payment; matching everyone's taste. Nudge (extrinsic): social proof — "see who else is going." Aha: "booked, paid, and everyone's in — in one thread." Today → gap: payment-in-chat + "who's going" exist but group-booking is still clunky → Play #2 (group booking + split pay).

C. The "I live here and I'm bored on Saturday" local — Job (functional): find a novel thing to do in my own city this weekend. (Personal): learn a skill / meet people. Friction: Airbnb isn't top-of-mind for local plans; app is coded as "travel." Nudge (intrinsic): novelty + belonging. Aha: "I met three people at a pottery class 10 minutes from home." Today → gap: 40% of Originals are already local S11 but there's no "near me this weekend" local mode → Play #3 (local weekend mode).

D. The "help me meet people" solo traveler / newcomer — Job (social/emotional): turn a solo trip (or a new city I moved to) into human connection without it being weird. Friction: fear of an awkward group; no easy way to see who else is coming. Nudge (intrinsic): belonging. Aha: "I showed up alone and left with a group chat." Today → gap: the social graph (see-who's-going, DMs, connections profile) is the seed S3 but under-productized as a meet-people value prop → Play #4 (connection graph).

E. The "give me a peak experience money can buy" splurge seeker — Job (personal/status): a once-in-a-lifetime, brag-worthy access (celebrity, exclusive venue). Friction: these are normally invitation-only / unavailable. Nudge (extrinsic): scarcity + status. Aha: "I spent a day with an athlete I idolize." Today → gap: Airbnb Originals is exactly this S3 but is a marketing/PR play, not yet a repeatable premium tier → Play #5 (Originals as a premium subscription/access tier).

F. The "host who wants to earn from a skill, not a room" supply-side segment — Job (functional): earn from expertise/passion without owning property. (Emotional): share what I love + be seen as great at it. Friction: 2023 pause bred distrust; unclear if demand exists; thin local liquidity. Nudge (extrinsic): earnings + the 4.93 rating badge. Aha: "my class is booked out every weekend." Today → gap: 60k applied but demand liquidity is uneven S11S2 → Play #6 (host demand-guarantee in launch cities).

8Strategy (Shreyas Strategy Template)

  • One-sentence strategy: Use curated, host-led local activities as Airbnb's top-of-funnel and higher-frequency layer — cheap, memorable first touches that convert new and local users into high-AOV home guests and, eventually, a defensible social + loyalty graph rivals can't build.
  • Prioritize: downstream stay-conversion, per-city liquidity, quality bar, the social/connection graph. Don't over-serve: near-term Experiences revenue, long-tail supply breadth, matching Viator's SKU count.
  • Pillars (moat → segment): (1) Funnel cross-sell → memorable-trip travelers (A) + splurge seekers (E); (2) Social graph → group organizers (B) + connection-seeking solos (D); (3) Local frequency → bored-locals (C); (4) Curated supply → skill-hosts (F).
  • North star: new + returning guests who complete an experience AND book a stay within 90 days (conversion-weighted, not raw seats).
  • Non-priorities (trade-offs): not chasing standalone Experiences GMV; not competing on SKU breadth vs. GetYourGuide; not building loyalty on stays yet (test on experiences first); not global-toggle rollouts.
  • Roadmap / metrics:
    • Now — deepen launch cities (Paris) to per-city PMF. Leading: experiences booked per active city / week. Lagging: 90-day experience→stay conversion.
    • Next — group booking + local weekend mode; productize the connection graph. Leading: % multiplayer bookings; local (unattached) share. Lagging: repeat-experience rate.
    • Later — Originals as premium access tier + loyalty (Delta miles → own program). Leading: miles-earning attach; premium tier sell-through. Lagging: frequency (experiences/user/year).

9Contrarian bets & open tensions

  • Bet: "spend $200M+ on a business we say won't make meaningful money soon." Bear: classic category-sprawl distraction; 2016 already failed; management won't share an attach rate ("we don't have numbers to share" S11) — a tell it's underperforming. Counter: the ROI is homes conversion (1-in-3 in 90 days S1) + a frequency gain + the least-AI-disruptable inventory; measured as CAC, the math can work even at ~$0 direct profit.
  • Bet: curate/purge supply instead of maximizing it. Bear: you starve liquidity in a marketplace that already lacks it. Counter: in experiences, one bad tour poisons trust more than a missing one — median quality > catalog size. S7
  • Bet: city-by-city, not global. Bear: painfully slow vs. Viator/GYG's global catalogs. Counter: experiences liquidity is inherently local; a global toggle with thin per-city supply is worse than nothing. S2
  • Best skeptic angle: the refusal to disclose attach rate + the blended "Nights & Seats" metric S1S11 suggests Experiences isn't yet clearing the bar Airbnb hoped — this is the third attempt, and management is managing optics.
  • Valuation tension: ABNB trades on the homes franchise + margins; Experiences is optionality the Street barely credits. Upside is unpriced; downside is mostly the ~$200M spend + focus cost. Asymmetric — but only if frequency actually inflects.

10Big Picture — Looks Wrong, Is Right

A. Wise refusals

  • Not chasing Experiences revenue / not disclosing an attach rate → right because the KPI is stay conversion, not experience GMV; forcing near-term monetization would kill the funnel value. Judge it as CAC, not a P&L. S1, S11
  • Not matching Viator/GetYourGuide on SKU count → right because breadth without a quality bar recreates the 2016 flop ("most experiences aren't unique" S8); Airbnb is betting curation + social + funnel beats catalog size.
  • Not launching everywhere at once → right because experiences liquidity is per-city-per-week; a global launch on thin local supply produces empty search results and dead trust. S2
  • Not building a homes loyalty program yet → right; test loyalty mechanics on cheap, frequent experience bookings (Delta miles) before betting the core business. S1

B. Counterintuitive moves

  • Deleting ~5,000 listings right before a "relaunch" → the bigger play: raise the median so the brand promise ("only-on-Airbnb") holds; supply you're embarrassed by is negative inventory. S7
  • Letting ~50% of bookings come from people not staying in a home → looks like the funnel is "leaking" to hotels/locals; actually it's the point — net-new audience + frequency beyond the big trip. S2
  • Booking a celebrity day (Originals) that can't scale → it's a brand/PR flywheel (13k press stories, 660M impressions S11) that acquires demand + supply far cheaper than paid marketing — the unscalable hero product funds the scalable long tail.
  • Treating the May release as "marketing," shipping features whenever ready → in the AI age, a big annual moment holds features back; continuous shipping + one showcase gets both velocity and narrative. S2

11Mistakes & Mis-executions → Opportunities

  • Weak native discovery / intent capture → why: Airbnb users arrive with lodging intent; "things to do in Paris Saturday" queries go to Google/Viator, not the Airbnb app → fix: own local-activity intent via SEO + an AI trip-planner that surfaces experiences before the stay is even booked. S1
  • Group/multiplayer booking still clunky despite "almost all bookings are multiplayer" being Airbnb's own stated insight S1 → why: the app was built single-booker for lodging; payment-in-chat is a patch, not a native group flow → fix: first-class group booking with split-pay and taste-matching (Play #2). (Author judgment — Airbnb hasn't admitted this as a gap.)
  • Third attempt with unresolved demand question → why: 2016 targeted a too-niche "authentic" bar with no mass appeal, then 2023 pause reset supply trust; demand-side intent was never solved, only supply quality S7S8 → fix: solve demand liquidity (local weekend mode, Play #3) not just supply curation.
  • Under-productized social graph → why: "see who's going" + connections profile shipped as features, not as a headline meet-people value prop; the strongest anti-OTA moat is treated as a nice-to-have S3 → fix: make "meet people" a first-class reason to book (Play #4).
  • Opacity as a management tell → why: blended "Nights & Seats" + "no numbers to share" on attach rate S1S11 signals the metric isn't ready — which erodes host/investor confidence in a business that needs belief to bootstrap → fix: publish a per-city liquidity + conversion scorecard to hosts (not the Street) to build supply-side trust. (Author judgment.)

12What's missing → Plays to run (10× / 100× — hypotheses, not today)

Gaps: (a) local-activity intent isn't captured (goes to Google/Viator); (b) group booking is native to the job but not the product; (c) the social graph is under-monetized; (d) locals are a proven-but-unowned audience; (e) Originals is a PR asset, not a repeatable revenue tier; (f) per-city supply liquidity is unguaranteed.

  • Play #1 — Trip-attached AI itinerary. Move: when a stay is booked, auto-assemble a personalized day-by-day plan of nearby experiences + services. Gap: weak discovery / low experience intent. Why Airbnb: it owns the trip dates, location, party size, and 500M+ reviews — no competitor has the stay context. 10×: converts passive lodging bookings into multi-experience trips, lifting frequency + attach. Proof-point: in Paris, offer a 1-tap "your Saturday" plan to homes guests; measure attach uplift.
  • Play #2 — Native group booking + split pay + taste-match. Move: book one experience for a group in a thread, everyone pays their share, suggestions ranked by the group's combined tastes. Gap: multiplayer is the job, single-player is the product. Why Airbnb: "who's going" + payment-in-chat already exist S3; it owns the identities. 10×: wins the organizer segment (B) — the highest-value, highest-frequency booker. Proof: split-pay on Originals in 3 cities.
  • Play #3 — Local Weekend Mode. Move: a "near me this weekend" surface that recodes Airbnb from travel-app to local-plans-app. Gap: locals are 40% of Originals S11 but unowned. Why Airbnb: brand + the largest local host network. 100× frequency: turns a twice-a-year app into a weekly one — the single biggest lever on the whole company's engagement. Proof: geofenced local push in Paris/LA; measure weekly active repeat.
  • Play #4 — The Connection Graph as the product. Move: make "meet people through experiences" the headline value; persistent group chats, "people you met," alumni events. Gap: social graph under-productized. Why Airbnb: only in-person Airbnb surface; OTAs can't replicate. 10× retention: social ties are stickier than any inventory. Proof: post-experience "stay in touch" group; measure rebook + referral.
  • Play #5 — Originals as a premium access tier / membership. Move: recurring premium tier for scarce celebrity/exclusive-venue access; a place to spend loyalty currency. Gap: Originals is PR, not revenue. Why Airbnb: brand pull that got 660M impressions S11. 10× monetization on a high-margin, high-status SKU + a loyalty sink. Proof: waitlist + premium price test on next Originals drop.
  • Play #6 — Host demand-guarantee in launch cities. Move: in a focus city, guarantee hosts a booking floor to seed liquidity, funded as CAC. Gap: per-city liquidity + host trust after 2023 pause. Why Airbnb: it controls demand routing + the $200M budget. 10× supply: solves cold-start the way it can afford to. Proof: Paris pilot with a booking guarantee; measure supply retention.

Small compounding wins: faster experience checkout (fewer taps); "book again / gift this" on completed experiences; reviews that auto-suggest the next experience; host quick-reply templates; map-native browse (Airbnb noted it's "not map-native" in chat — but its own app should be S1); calendar sync so experiences slot into the trip itinerary; "add a friend" one-tap on any booking.

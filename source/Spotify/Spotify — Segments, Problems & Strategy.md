## 0. Positioning — the market map

**Axes:** catalog strategy (licensed-neutral aggregator ↔ owned/exclusive content) × monetization model (single-price access ↔ power-law tiered pricing). These are creative, not vanity (not price-vs-quality) — chosen because they're the two dimensions Spotify itself keeps making explicit trade-offs on (refusing exclusivity in §10, building the tiering roadmap in §12).

| | Licensed-neutral | Owned/exclusive |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-price / bundled** | Apple Music, Amazon Music (bundled into hardware/Prime ecosystems, one price, no tiering push) | *(no major audio player here — this is Netflix's video quadrant, not audio)* |
| **Power-law / tiered** | **Spotify** — Audiobooks+, Reserved, per-market pricing, tiering roadmap ("more in the next 11 months") | *(empty — a content-owner refusing tiering would leave money on the table; no one occupies this for audio)* |

**The empty space Spotify is claiming:** licensed-neutral aggregator that prices like a power-law SaaS company. Apple can't easily follow without disrupting its bundle-first ecosystem economics (Apple Music is a loss-leader for hardware, not a standalone P&L optimized this way). Amazon Music is in the same bundled-loss-leader position via Prime. A content-owner (hypothetical audio-Netflix) can't follow without abandoning the label-neutrality that gets it the full catalog in the first place. This is why the position is defensible, not just occupied.

## 1. Segmentation basis (demand side)

Needs axes: **Function** (Schäfer: mood-regulation / self-awareness / social-relatedness) × **Involvement** (casual lean-back ↔ power lean-in) × **Context** (commute/workout/study/sleep/social). Needs-based, not demographic — per Shreyas's explicit trap (composite personas, age/geo segments rejected).

## 2b. Partner segments (supply side — Spotify is two-sided)

Spotify's supply side: rights holders who provide the catalog, and advertisers who provide the ad-side revenue. Segmenting supply the same needs-based way (not by size) surfaces which supply relationship to invest in differently.

| Supply segment | Their need | Friction (theirs) | Accelerate supply? | Incentive to invest? | Helps adoption? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Independent artists / self-releasers** (Spotify for Artists) | Discovery, direct fan monetization, transparent payout data | Buried in a 250M-track catalog; payout thresholds | **High** — the long-tail volume engine | **High** — new tools (marketplace, artist-services) = new revenue for them | Moderate — long-tail, not headline draw, but differentiates vs. majors-only rivals |
| **Advertisers (brand + programmatic/DSP)** | Reach an engaged, logged-in, first-party audience; measurable ROI | Audio-only creative historically weak; Spotify was late to programmatic (§11 admitted mistake) | Moderate, growing via Amazon/Yahoo DSP | Rising — ad GM +441bps makes this segment more attractive to court | High — biddable inventory growth funds the H2'26 ad re-acceleration bet |
| **Audiobook publishers** | New revenue channel beyond Audible's incumbency | Catalog rights negotiation, competing incumbent | High — catalog 150K→700K titles fast | High — Audiobooks+ hit ~$100M ARR in <1yr, proven wedge | High — fastest-proven adoption driver of the three newer segments |
| **Major labels (UMG, Sony, Warner)** | Maximize per-stream payout, control release strategy | Spotify's per-stream economics vs. their alternative leverage | High (already near-full catalog) | Moderate — UMG's AI-remix deal shows selective co-investment appetite | High — their catalog IS the core product |
| **Podcast creators/networks** | Distribution reach, ad monetization, cross-promo with music audience | Trust still rebuilding post 2019-22 exclusivity overspend (§11) | Moderate — open marketplace now, not exclusive | Rebuilding | High — video podcast +50% YoY is a real engagement driver |

**Prioritization (accelerate-supply × incentive-to-invest × help-adoption):**
1. **Independent artists** — court first. Feeds Play #1 (superfan commerce) and Play #4 (artist-services SaaS) directly; high on all three scores.
2. **Advertisers (programmatic)** — must-invest, not a differentiator yet. Spotify is catching up, not leading; the H2'26 promise depends on this segment specifically.
3. **Audiobook publishers** — already working; keep courting, lower marginal urgency than #1/#2.
4. **Major labels** — table-stakes to manage (this is the named #1 weakness in Dossier §5 — "supplier power" — not a segment Spotify can choose to prioritize differently; it's a constraint).
5. **Podcast creators** — moderate priority; still earning back trust, real but not the fastest lever right now.

## 3. Strategy (Strategy Template)

- **Positioning:** licensed-neutral aggregator monetizing like a power-law SaaS pricer (see §0) — the uncontested quadrant.
- **One-sentence strategy:** own the world's listening at free-tier scale via mood-regulation, then monetize the most-engaged needs (fandom, discovery, social) and the highest-leverage supply segment (independent artists) with higher-margin add-ons that route around the label tax.
- **Target segments — prioritize:** Mood-Regulation (demand scale) + Fandom/Discovery/Social (demand monetization) + Independent artists + Advertisers (supply). **Don't over-serve:** passive radio-replacers unlikely to convert; major labels beyond table-stakes maintenance (limited leverage there regardless of investment).
- **Pillars:** taste model (LTM) → mood + discovery; verified-fandom graph → fandom + artist supply; multi-vertical stacking → retention across all demand segments; ubiquity/device graph → mood + focus; artist-services/marketplace tools → independent-artist supply.
- **Non-priorities:** owning content, competing on catalog breadth, a fixed price-hike cadence, chasing ad revenue over ad margin before H2'26, out-competing specialists in Focus (accept the gap or partner rather than build head-on).
- **Roadmap:** *Now* — context-sensing DJ + Reserved presales + independent-artist tooling attach. *Next* — programmatic ad scale-up + discovery/social depth. *Later* — functional-audio vertical (close the Focus gap) + taste-as-platform licensing.

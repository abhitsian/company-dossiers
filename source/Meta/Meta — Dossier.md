## 1. Business anatomy — Acquire / Engage / Retain / Monetize
*The full mechanics, trivial included.*

**Acquire**
- Organic/viral is the acquisition channel: the network *is* the funnel — four 2B-scale apps feed each other, so new surfaces ride the existing graph instead of acquiring cold.
- Regional split within the graph: WhatsApp is the primary Meta AI driver in India/Indonesia; Facebook drives it in the U.S. [S2]
- New surfaces ride the graph: Threads, and the standalone Meta AI app — "consistently near the top of the app stores" [S1].
- Hardware opens a *new* acquisition surface: glasses as a beachhead for always-on AI, a user context the phone-based graph can't reach [S3].

**Engage**
- Core loop = ranked feed/Reels driven by the recommendation engine; the aha is content that feels made-for-you.
- AI-translated/dubbed video (>0.5B weekly [S1]) grows the pool of content across languages.
- Same-day posts are now >30% of recommended Reels, 2x a year ago [S1] — freshness drives return frequency.
- AI-generated *personalized* content is the next hook [S1].
- Meta AI is moving from an "invoked tool" to an "always-on service" on glasses [S3].

**Retain**
- Switching costs are the social graph plus message history (WhatsApp/Messenger) — the boring plumbing that makes leaving expensive.
- Retention risk is external, not competitive: the QoQ DAU dip was outages plus a Russia block [S1], not churn.
- RL/glasses add a *hardware* lock-in layer (Neural Band, Horizon worlds) on top of the software graph.

**Monetize**
- Ads are ~98% of revenue and "by far the most important driver for the next couple of years" [S2].
- Growth levers move together: impressions +19% and price/ad +12% *at the same time* in Q1'26 [S1] — volume and yield both up.
- A stack of ad-AI products each at multibillion run-rates: value-optimization >$20B ARR, partnership ads $10B, video-gen tools $10B, incremental attribution multibillion [S1][S2].
- Emerging line: FoA-Other +74% on WhatsApp paid messaging + subscriptions [S1].
- Emerging line: business AIs — 10M weekly convos, unmonetized, commissions/premium/high-compute tiers planned [S1].
- Emerging line: WhatsApp Status ads.
- Emerging line: hardware unit sales (glasses at $379–$799 [S3]).

## 3. Wow Vault ★
*The selective, non-obvious layer — what makes an interviewer lean in.*

**★ Redistributing ads beat adding ads — by 4x**
- **Mechanism:** In H2'25, showing Facebook ads to the right users and sessions (rather than raising how many ads people see) "delivered a nearly 4x larger revenue impact than Facebook ad load increases" [S2]. The lever is allocation, not supply: put the ad in front of the person most likely to buy, at the right moment.
- **Why non-obvious:** Most people model ad revenue as impressions × price and assume growth means more ad slots. Meta's own data says targeting precision has ~4x the impact of adding supply.
- **Deploy:** Any "how would you grow ad revenue" / monetization metrics question — recall hook: *"redistribution beat ad-load 4-to-1."*
- **Source:** [S2]

**★ The first recommendation architecture that scales like an LLM**
- **Mechanism:** "This is the first time we have found a recommendation model architecture that can scale with similar efficiency as LLMs" [S2] — a new sequence-learning design lets ranking models grow "while preserving an attractive ROI." Historically, bigger recommenders hit a cost wall when serving predictions.
- **Why non-obvious:** People assume "scaling laws" belong to chatbots. Meta ported them to the ranking engine that prints the money — the highest-ROI use of scaling laws in the industry.
- **Deploy:** "Where does AI actually create value" / moat questions — recall hook: *"scaling laws, but for the feed."*
- **Source:** [S2]

**★ Big models don't serve — they teach**
- **Mechanism:** GEM (their giant model) is "too costly for inference"; the way they get value "is by using them to transfer knowledge to smaller lightweight models used at run time" [S2]. In Q1'26 the *ad* recommender flipped this: a 1-trillion-parameter model used *for inference*, "co-designed with the underlying silicon" to hold sub-second latency, that "intelligently routes requests to more compute-intensive inference models when it determines there is a higher probability of conversion" [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** Two-tier design: distill the giant into a cheap student for the common case, and escalate to the expensive teacher only when the odds of a sale justify the compute. Inference ROI becomes a product decision, not just an infra one.
- **Deploy:** Any AI-cost / infra-ROI / "how do you afford this" question — recall hook: *"escalate compute only when the click is likely."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ "AI should serve people, not sit in a data center automating society"**
- **Mechanism:** Zuckerberg's contrarian thesis: "AI is going to amplify people's ability… People will be more important in the future, not less" — rejecting a "centralized thing that does all the productive work" [S1]. The proof he offers: "I don't hear any other labs out there talking about how they're building an AI that's really good at shopping" [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** While other labs race toward automation and dev-tools, Meta positions consumer empowerment (shopping, social, local, games) as the differentiator, and uses *shopping competence* as the test that its strategy is genuinely different.
- **Deploy:** "How is Meta's AI strategy different from OpenAI/Google" — recall hook: *"nobody else is bragging about shopping."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ Self-improvement ≠ coding**
- **Mechanism:** "People conflate coding with self-improvement more than they should. Coding is one ingredient… not the only thing" — framed as "table stakes," not a pivot to dev tools [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** The market equates "recursive self-improvement" with code-writing agents. Meta separates them: its self-improvement loop runs on the ranking and recommendation stack, not GitHub.
- **Deploy:** AI-strategy / "what is their real advantage" — recall hook: *"coding is one ingredient, not the loop."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ The "give it to my mother" quality bar**
- **Mechanism:** "There aren't that many [agents] that I would want to give to my mother… getting to that quality bar is something I care about more than hitting a specific week for launching" [S1]. Contrasted with rough systems like OpenClaw, which "requires local install + terminal config, addressable to only hundreds of thousands / millions vs. billions" [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** It turns Meta's distribution moat (3.5B+ DAU) into a *quality constraint*: you can't ship a terminal-config toy to billions of non-technical people, so consumer-grade polish is the real barrier, and the thing Meta is forced to solve.
- **Deploy:** Product-sense / "why is consumer AI hard" — recall hook: *"would you give it to your mother?"*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ Glasses are the only form factor where an AI can see what you see**
- **Mechanism:** "Glasses are the only form factor where you can let an AI see what you see, hear what you hear, talk to you throughout the day and very soon generate whatever UI you need right in your vision in real time" [S3]. The endgame: not apps, but **AI-generated UI rendered on demand** in your field of view.
- **Why non-obvious:** It moves the glasses bet from "wearable gadget" to "the input/output surface for personal superintelligence." The phone can't do it because it can't share your sensory context all day.
- **Deploy:** "Why is Meta spending on hardware" / platform-strategy — recall hook: *"AI needs eyes; only glasses have them."*
- **Source:** [S3]

**★ The neural band replaces the entire input stack**
- **Mechanism:** The Meta Neural Band reads "signals from your brain with little muscle movements"; Zuckerberg typed silently at "about 30 words a minute" on stage [S3]. He framed it as the next input shift: "Every new computing platform has a new way to interact with it" [S3].
- **Why non-obvious:** Every platform transition (mouse → multitouch) is defined by its input method. Meta is claiming the next one — a wristband that reads muscle signals (EMG) — and shipping it at $799 with the Display, not in a research lab.
- **Deploy:** Platform-shift / hardware-strategy — recall hook: *"30 words a minute, typed with no keyboard."*
- **Source:** [S3]

**★ Business AIs went 10x in one year, and it's still free**
- **Mechanism:** Business-AI conversations grew from "1M at start of year" to ">10M weekly" [S1] (up from "over 1 million weekly" the prior quarter [S2]) — "currently free, monetization model to come (commissions / premium / high-compute tiers)" [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** The classic Meta playbook, mid-execution: build usage to huge scale *before* turning on monetization. The 10x growth curve at zero price is the leading indicator of the next FoA-Other revenue engine.
- **Deploy:** Monetization / "where's the next revenue line" — recall hook: *"1M → 10M weekly, and they haven't charged a cent yet."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ First-principles content understanding — the end of "statistical patterns"**
- **Mechanism:** "For the first time in Meta's history, we're going to be able to develop a first principles understanding of what you care about and what each piece of content… is about" — moving past "statistical patterns of what types of people engage with what content," plus **generating personalized content created specifically for users** [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** The feed has always been correlational ("people like you clicked this"). Understanding *content itself* by meaning, plus generating custom content, is a categorical shift, and the reason ad targeting keeps outrunning a "mature" market.
- **Deploy:** "How can a saturated ad business keep growing double the industry" — recall hook: *"from correlation to comprehension."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ Cinema-beating brightness — the "stereo ubiquity" thesis**
- **Mechanism:** James Cameron on stage: Quest hits "30-foot lamberts equivalent" vs. best theaters at 16, most at 3 — "an order of magnitude brighter"; he claims "~20% more engagement" from stereo 3D and calls episodic TV, not movies, "the low-hanging fruit people have historically ignored" [S3].
- **Why non-obvious:** The VR content pitch is usually about immersion. Cameron pitches raw physics (brightness) and picks *TV*, not blockbusters, as the wedge — a specific, defensible content strategy hiding inside a hardware keynote.
- **Deploy:** "Is VR content a real business" — recall hook: *"the headset out-brightens the best theater by 10x."*
- **Source:** [S3]

## 4. Reframes & mental models to borrow
*The company's own framing devices, restated so you can wield them on any prompt.*

- **"Redistribution beats ad load."** Growth from allocating existing supply better, not adding supply → monetization & metrics questions on any marketplace/feed. [S2]
- **"Big models teach, small models serve."** Spend the giant model's cost once to train cheap students used at runtime → AI-cost / infra-ROI questions. [S2] The 2026 variant: escalate to the expensive model only when the payoff (a sale) is likely [S1].
- **"AI amplifies people, it doesn't replace them."** Product bets should raise individual capability (shopping, creating) rather than automate labor → strategy/positioning questions. [S1]
- **"Coding is one ingredient, not the loop."** Don't equate recursive self-improvement with code agents → AI-strategy questions. [S1]

- **"Meta is a deep technology company."** Frontier AI "won't always be available through an API" (competitive + safety), so you must *own the model* to build the experience → build-vs-buy / vertical-integration questions. [S2]
- **"Format evolution: text → photo → video → an explosion of new media formats."** Each format shift resets engagement; glasses are "the ultimate incarnation" → platform-timing questions. [S2][S3]
- **"AI-generated UI on demand."** The endgame isn't apps; it's interfaces rendered into your vision as needed → future-of-computing questions. [S3]
- **"Presence, lost to phones, regained with glasses."** Frame the product problem as an emotional job (presence), not a spec → product-design / vision questions. [S3]

## 6. AI impact — deflationary or moat?

| AI vector | Deflationary | Moat-deepening |
|---|---|---|
| Content generation (feed/ads) | Generative content becomes cheap & universal — everyone gets it | **Yes** — applied to Meta's proprietary engagement graph + first-principles content model [S1], rivals can't replicate the signal |
| Ad targeting / ranking | — | **Yes** — LLM-scale recommender co-designed with owned silicon [S1]; the data + compute are the moat |
| AI assistants (Meta AI) | Assistant capability is commoditizing across labs | **Mixed** — distribution to billions [S1] is the moat, not the model; but model quality is a live gap [S2] |
| Business/agentic AI | Agent frameworks commoditizing (OpenClaw et al. [S1]) | **Yes if** consumer-grade quality + distribution win ("give it to my mother" bar [S1]) |
| Coding productivity | Universal (+30%/engineer is table stakes [S2]) | **Neutral** — helps everyone; Meta's edge is applying it at 3.5B-user scale |
| Compute efficiency | Chips commoditize | **Yes** — custom silicon efficiency as "a strategic advantage over time" [S1] |
| Glasses + generated UI | — | **Yes** — a form factor only Meta is shipping at scale [S3]; hard to fast-follow |

**Net read:** Net **tailwind**. Meta is one of the few consumer companies where AI compounds a proprietary asset (the engagement + ads data flywheel) instead of commoditizing it, and it owns the full stack (silicon → model → distribution). **The one real AI risk to watch:** the model-quality gap. Everything rests on the assumption that owning the frontier model matters; MSL is ~6–10 months old and its first outputs were "unfulfilling" [S2]. If a rival's assistant becomes clearly better *and* reaches consumers before Meta closes the gap, the "own the model" thesis weakens and the $125–145B/yr bet [S1] looks stranded.

## 7. Needs-based segments → problems → solutions
*Shreyas method: segment on NEEDS, not demographics. Passes the 5-Point Test.*

**Segmentation basis:** The needs axes are **(a) what job the user hires Meta's surface for** (be entertained, stay connected, create, sell/reach customers) and **(b) their tolerance for AI mediation**. These are product-specific, targetable via surface, and each maps to a distinct monetization path — never a demographic cut.

**A. The Passive Consumer — "fill my idle minutes"**
- **Job:** Functional = kill time / be entertained; Emotional = low-effort dopamine. **Friction:** running out of relevant content; language walls. **Nudge:** extrinsic (autoplay, freshness). **Aha:** *"how does it always know what I want to watch?"* **Today → gap:** Reels + AI-dubbing (>0.5B weekly [S1]) already serve this well; the gap is *supply* of fresh personalized content. → **Play #1** (generate bespoke content).

**B. The Connector — "keep me present with the people I care about"**
- **Job:** Social = maintain relationships; Emotional = presence, belonging. **Friction:** phones "lost us presence" [S3]; group coordination is scattered. **Nudge:** intrinsic (relationships). **Aha:** *"I felt like they were in the room."* **Today → gap:** WhatsApp/Messenger own messaging, but presence is still 2D; glasses' conversation-focus + AI translation aim here [S3]. → **Play #4** (presence layer via glasses).

**C. The Creator — "help me make and get paid"**
- **Job:** Functional = produce content + earn; Social = grow an audience. **Friction:** 3D/immersive content is "really hard" to make [S3]; monetization is thin. **Nudge:** extrinsic (commissions, reach). **Aha:** *"I prompted a world and it built it."* **Today → gap:** affiliate/product-tagging in test [S1]; Horizon Studio's agentic assistant stitches generation tools [S3], but creator payout depth is early. → **Play #2** (creator commerce) + **Play #5** (prompt-to-world).

**D. The Advertiser / SMB — "get me ROI-positive customers"**
- **Job:** Functional = acquire customers profitably. **Friction:** targeting precision, creative production cost, attribution trust. **Nudge:** extrinsic (measurable ROI — Li's whole budgeting-process answer [S2]). **Aha:** *"the incremental conversions actually showed up."* **Today → gap:** the deep ad-AI stack already serves this (value-opt >$20B ARR [S1]); the gap is *agentic* end-to-end campaign automation. → **Play #3** (autonomous ad agent).

**E. The AI-First User / Shopper — "just handle it for me"**
- **Job:** Functional = get answers, buy things, complete tasks; Personal = save effort. **Friction:** assistants that don't reach consumer-quality ("give it to my mother" [S1]); no native commerce loop. **Nudge:** intrinsic (convenience) + extrinsic (deals). **Aha:** *"it found and bought it without me leaving the chat."* **Today → gap:** Meta AI leads in shopping intent [S1] but commerce isn't closed; business AIs unmonetized [S1]. → **Play #6** (close the shopping loop).

## 8. Strategy *(Shreyas Strategy Template)*

- **One-sentence strategy:** Use the ad monopoly's cash to become the vertically-integrated owner of personal AI — model, silicon, and the glasses form factor — so that when computing shifts from phones to always-on AI, Meta owns the platform instead of renting it.
- **Prioritize / Don't over-serve:** Prioritize consumer-grade quality + distribution to billions and the ads flywheel that funds everything. Don't over-serve developer/agent tooling ("coding is one ingredient" [S1]) or precise near-term ROI forecasting ("no precise plan for how each product scales" [S1]).
- **Pillars (moat → segment):** (1) Recommendation/ads AI flywheel → Advertisers + Passive Consumers; (2) Owned model + silicon → all AI surfaces; (3) Glasses + Neural Band → Connectors + AI-First users.
- **North star:** Time/engagement across the family → converted to ad yield; longer-term, Meta AI sessions per user [S1] and glasses DAU [S1] as leading indicators of the platform shift.
- **Non-priorities (trade-offs):** Buybacks (paused — "highest order priority… investing to lead in AI" [S2]); near-term RL profitability (losses at "peak," accepted [S2]); headcount growth (RIF in May, teams kept "leaner" as AI shrinks team size [S1]); committing to product launch dates ("quality over hitting a specific date" [S1]).
- **Roadmap / metrics:** **Now** — ads-AI stack scaling (leading: conversion-rate lift +1.6% [S1]; lagging: ad revenue +33% [S1]). **Next** — Meta AI + business AIs to scale, then monetize (leading: sessions/user double-digit % up, 10M weekly business convos [S1]; lagging: FoA-Other revenue). **Later** — glasses as platform (leading: AI-glasses DAU tripled [S1]; lagging: RL revenue turning up + hardware margin).

## 9. Contrarian bets & open tensions

- **Bet: spend $125–145B/yr on compute against unquantified returns.** *Bear:* no ROI signposts, "continued to underestimate compute needs" [S2], the $107B commitment increase [S1] locks in cost before revenue. *Counter:* the historical "build to billions then monetize" formula has worked every prior cycle, and the ads-AI stack is *already* paying off (impressions +19% & price +12% together [S1]).
- **Bet: own the frontier model rather than use an API.** *Bear:* MSL is ~6–10 months old, first models "unfulfilling" [S2]; Meta could burn billions to reach parity, not lead. *Counter:* "frontier AI won't always be available via API" for competitive + safety reasons [S2]; owning it is the only way to build the experiences.
- **Bet: glasses are the next platform.** *Bear:* stage demos glitched repeatedly (WhatsApp call, subtitles, music all failed [S3]); all-day AI is battery-limited to 1–2 hrs [S3]; RL revenue is *shrinking* [S1][S2]. *Counter:* AI-glasses DAU tripled Y/Y [S1] and it's the only form factor that gives AI your senses [S3].
- **Bet: shrink the company while spending more than ever.** *Bear:* the RIF + "leaner teams" [S1] mid-platform-shift could bleed talent when execution matters most. *Counter:* AI genuinely shrinks team size ("1–2 people in a week vs. dozens in months" [S1]; +30% output/engineer [S2]).
- **Best skeptic angle:** The whole thesis rests on Meta *catching up* on model quality while funding the largest CapEx ramp in its history, and management won't commit to a frontier-model timeline [S2] or per-product ROI [S1]. You're asked to trust the formula, not the numbers.
- **Valuation tension:** *(Price not disclosed in sources — do not fabricate.)* Ex-tax-benefit EPS was $7.31 [S1]; the market must price a company whose reported earnings are inflated by a one-off $8.03B tax benefit [S1] and whose FCF ($12.4B [S1]) is being outspent ~1.6x by CapEx ($19.8B [S1]).

## 10. Big Picture — Looks Wrong, Is Right

**A. Wise refusals** — what critics say Meta *should* do, but restraint is strategically right.

- **Won't give precise per-product ROI on CapEx** → correct, because the historical value came from building usage to billions *before* the monetization model existed (business AIs at 10M weekly convos, still free [S1]); committing to a monetization schedule now would force premature, worse monetization. [S1]
- **Won't commit to a frontier-model launch date** → "quality over hitting a specific date," and the "give it to my mother" bar [S1] means shipping a rough model to 3.5B people is worse than shipping late. Announcing a date creates pressure toward a bad launch. [S1]
- **Won't chase agentic/dev-tool AI like rivals** → "coding is one ingredient, not the loop" [S1]; Meta's asset is consumer distribution + engagement data, not developer mindshare. Copying OpenAI's positioning would abandon its actual moat. [S1]
- **Won't resume buybacks** → returning cash now would signal it has run out of high-ROI reinvestment exactly when the platform is shifting; pausing buybacks is the honest expression of "we are all-in" [S2].
- **Won't ship a terminal-config power-user agent (à la OpenClaw)** → addressable to "hundreds of thousands / millions vs. billions" [S1]; for Meta, a product that can't reach non-technical users at scale isn't a product. The refusal *is* the distribution strategy.

**B. Counterintuitive moves** — what looks like a mistake but serves a bigger play.

- **Laying people off while spending record amounts** → not cost panic; AI genuinely shrinks team size ("1–2 people, one week, vs. dozens, months" [S1]), so a leaner org is the rational response to its own productivity gains, not a contradiction of the growth story. [S1]
- **Pouring billions into Reality Labs while its revenue shrinks (−2%, −12%)** [S1][S2] → RL isn't a product line, it's the option on owning the *next* platform; measuring it on current revenue misreads it as a business instead of a bet. [S3]
- **Building "an AI that's really good at shopping"** [S1] → sounds trivial next to "superintelligence," but it's the deliberate proof that Meta's consumer-empowerment strategy is "extremely different" from rivals, and shopping is the shortest path from assistant to a commerce take-rate. [S1]
- **Buying $107B of contractual compute commitments in a single quarter** [S1] → looks reckless, but locking in supply (with cost/GW declining over time [S2]) while "capacity constrained through much of 2026" [S2] is how you avoid being the one who *couldn't* scale.
- **Spending inference budget dynamically per-request** [S1] → looks like over-engineering; it's the mechanism that makes a trillion-parameter ad model affordable to serve — quality *and* ROI from the same design.

## 11. Mistakes & Mis-executions → Opportunities

- **Repeatedly underestimating compute needs** (management-admitted: Li, "we have continued to underestimate our compute needs" [S2]) → *why:* demand for AI ranking/ads outran planning models faster than expected [S2]; forecasting a novel S-curve from a linear base. → *opportunity:* the same under-forecasting means the ads-AI upside is likely *under*-guided too — the CapEx raise [S1] is a tell that the revenue engine is running hotter than the outlook admits. [S1][S2]
- **Frontier-model program started late / behind** (MSL only ~6 months old in Q4, first models "unfulfilling," "showing trajectory rather than a single moment" [S2]) → *why:* Meta prioritized open-weight/recommendation AI and was slower to stand up a superintelligence lab than OpenAI/Google. → *opportunity:* MSL + Muse Spark [S1] is the catch-up; if distribution (3.5B DAU) can carry a *merely-competitive* model to consumer wins, Meta doesn't need to *lead* on benchmarks — the fix is distribution-led, not benchmark-led. [S1][S2]
- **Hardware reliability shown to be fragile** — at Connect, live demos failed repeatedly (WhatsApp call, music, subtitles: "This WiFi is brutal" [S3]) → *why:* pushing bleeding-edge glasses (Neural Band, Live AI) onto a live stage before the stack was demo-stable. → *opportunity:* the visible glitches undercut the platform narrative; getting all-day reliable Live AI (battery-limited to 1–2 hrs today [S3]) is the single unlock that would convert skeptics — a concrete, ownable milestone. [S3]
- **Monetization consistently trails usage** — business AIs at 10M weekly convos still free, monetization "further out" [S1]; personal agents unmonetized [S1] → *why:* deliberate "scale-first" doctrine, but it means a growing share of engagement generates $0 while CapEx compounds. → *opportunity:* even a modest commission/premium tier on business AIs [S1] is a near-term, high-margin FoA-Other accelerant that's being left on the table — a fast lever if growth-rate pressure hits. [S1]
- **Reality Labs revenue actively declining** (−2% Q1, −12% Q4 [S1][S2]) while it's positioned as the future → *why:* Quest headset sales fading faster than AI-glasses can offset [S1]; the VR generation is aging out before the glasses generation scales. → *opportunity:* concentrate spend behind glasses (management is already redirecting "most investment toward glasses and wearables" [S2]) and stop reporting RL as a P&L segment publicly — the mixed number muddies an otherwise clean option. [S1][S2]
- **DAU exposed to single-country/outage shocks** (QoQ dip from Iran outages + Russia WhatsApp block [S1]) → *why:* concentration in fragile-connectivity, high-regulation markets. → *opportunity:* the fragility is geopolitical, not product; it argues for the glasses/hardware line as a *durable-hardware* moat less exposed to a single government's block. [S1]

## 12. What's missing → Plays to run *(10× / 100× — hypotheses, not today)*

**Gaps first:** (1) Enormous engagement generates *personalized-content supply* only from other users — Meta doesn't yet generate the content itself at scale. (2) Business AIs (10M weekly convos) are entirely unmonetized [S1]. (3) The assistant leads in shopping *intent* but the purchase loop isn't closed [S1]. (4) Presence is still 2D despite owning messaging. (5) Creating immersive content is "really hard" [S3], throttling the Horizon ecosystem. (6) Ad creative + campaign management is AI-assisted but not yet autonomous.

- **Play #1 — Generative personalized content feed.** *Move:* generate bespoke content ("personalized content created specifically for users" [S1]) to supplement human posts. *Gap:* content-supply ceiling on engagement. *Why Meta:* owns the first-principles content-understanding model [S1] + the ranking engine + the audience. *10× or 100×:* 100× — near-infinite, per-user content supply removes the human-supply constraint entirely. *First proof-point:* AI-generated Reels interstitials for a cohort, measure incremental watch time.
- **Play #2 — Creator commerce take-rate.** *Move:* scale affiliate/product-tagging [S1] into a full creator storefront with payouts. *Gap:* thin creator monetization. *Why Meta:* the tagging tests + the shopping-competent assistant + billions of buyers. *10×:* a new creator-economy revenue line. *Proof-point:* commission revenue per tagged Reel in the current test partners [S1].
- **Play #3 — Autonomous ad agent.** *Move:* turn the ad-AI stack (value-opt, video-gen, incremental attribution [S1][S2]) into one agent that plans, generates, targets, and optimizes a campaign end-to-end for an SMB. *Gap:* today it's tools, not an agent. *Why Meta:* it already owns every component at multibillion run-rate. *10×:* cuts SMB ad production cost → expands the advertiser base. *Proof-point:* an SMB spends $X via a single prompt, measure ROAS vs. manual.
- **Play #4 — Presence layer on glasses.** *Move:* productize conversation-focus + AI translation [S3] into a "be present anywhere" experience. *Gap:* presence lost to phones [S3]. *Why Meta:* owns messaging + the glasses + Neural Band. *10×:* a new social primitive beyond the 2D feed. *Proof-point:* retention lift among glasses users who use conversation-focus weekly.
- **Play #5 — Prompt-to-world creation.** *Move:* ship Horizon Studio's agentic assistant [S3] as consumer prompt-to-world, shareable in feed [S2]. *Gap:* 3D content is "really hard" to make [S3]. *Why Meta:* the engine (4x faster load, 5x concurrency [S3]) + the AI generation tools + feed distribution. *100×:* turns every user into a world-builder, seeding the metaverse ecosystem. *Proof-point:* worlds-created-per-day after prompt-to-world beta.
- **Play #6 — Close the shopping loop.** *Move:* let Meta AI complete purchases in-chat (the shopping strength [S1] → transaction). *Gap:* intent without a closed loop; business AIs unmonetized [S1]. *Why Meta:* assistant leads in shopping + business AIs at 10M weekly convos + WhatsApp commerce rails. *100×:* a commerce take-rate across billions of conversations — the largest untapped revenue pool named in the sources. *Proof-point:* completed in-chat purchases in the LatAm/Indonesia SMB business-AI markets [S1].

**Small compounding wins (a dozen 5%s = a double):** same-day-post freshness in Reels (already 2x'd to >30% [S1]); AI-dubbing more languages (>0.5B weekly [S1]); Status ads density creep ("low in the near term" [S2] — headroom); Threads ad rollout to remaining markets [S2]; WhatsApp paid-messaging expansion ($2B run rate [S2]); off-site conversion coverage (+1.6% [S1]); routing more ad requests to the escalation model when a sale is likely [S1]; MTIA into training to cut inference cost [S2].

## 13. Interview arsenal

**A. The Deconstruct Set**

**1. Why this company?**
Meta inverts the "mature social network" read — it's the highest-margin machine in tech (41% operating margins on $56.3B in quarterly revenue) using an ad monopoly to fund vertical integration into the next computing platform: owned model, owned silicon, owned form factor (glasses) (§8 one-sentence strategy). The distinctive proof point is the compute architecture in §3: a trillion-parameter ad model too costly to run everywhere, so it's distilled into cheap students and escalated to the expensive tier only when a sale is likely — AI deepening a proprietary flywheel instead of commoditizing it. The bull case rests on one open bet: can distribution to 3.5B+ people carry a merely-competitive model to the win, or does Meta need model parity it doesn't have yet (§9)?

**2. What do you like about it?**
The "redistribution beats ad load" finding (§3) — targeting precision has roughly 4x the revenue impact of adding ad supply, a genuinely counterintuitive answer to "how do you grow an ad business." The distillation-plus-escalation inference design (§3, §5): a 1-trillion-parameter model that only spends the expensive compute tier when conversion odds justify it, so cost and quality are solved by the same architecture. Real, deepening moats in §5 — distribution at 3.5B+ DAU that a rival can't buy, and a recommendation engine that now scales like an LLM. And the "give it to my mother" quality bar (§3), which turns the distribution moat into a forcing function for consumer-grade polish that a smaller lab never has to clear.

**3. What don't you like about it?**
~98% ad concentration funding a $125–145B/yr CapEx bet (§5) — one revenue engine underwrites the entire platform wager. The frontier model is unproven: MSL is only ~6–10 months old and its first outputs were called "unfulfilling" (§5, §11). CapEx has no ROI signposts — Zuckerberg has said there's "no very precise plan for how each product scales month-over-month," and management has twice admitted underestimating compute needs (§5, §11). And hardware reliability is shown to be fragile, not theoretical — live Connect demos glitched repeatedly on stage (§11).

**4. How would you fix that?**
The ad-concentration risk has a near-term lever already sitting unused: business AIs carry 10M weekly conversations and are still free (§11, §12 gap #2) — even a modest commission or premium tier is a fast, high-margin FoA-Other accelerant. The model gap doesn't need a benchmark win to close: if 3.5B-scale distribution can carry a merely-competitive model to consumer wins, the fix is distribution-led, not benchmark-led (§11). And the hardware-fragility problem has one concrete, ownable milestone — get Live AI reliable all-day (it's battery-limited to 1–2 hrs today), which is the single unlock that would convert stage-demo skeptics into believers (§9, §11).

**5. How would you compete with it?**
Don't attack distribution (3.5B+ DAU, structurally unwinnable) or the ads stack (already multibillion-run-rate, still improving ~4x on targeting alone per §3). Attack the one moat the dossier's own table calls "Contested": the frontier model (§5). MSL is ~6–10 months old and its first outputs were "unfulfilling" (§5, §11) — the dossier names the exact play itself: "OpenAI/Google ship a clearly better assistant before Meta closes the gap" (§5). Aim that better assistant at the one segment where model quality, not distribution, is the deciding factor — the AI-First User/Shopper (§7E), whose job is "just handle it for me" and whose named friction is precisely that assistants haven't reached consumer quality. Meta's own business AIs are still free and its shopping loop still isn't closed (§7E, §12 gap #3) — so a rival that ships a genuinely better assistant *and* closes the purchase loop first gets to define "does this actually work" before Meta's model matures. The one structural advantage working against Meta here: it can't rush this. It has to clear its own "give it to my mother" bar (§3) before shipping anything to its full base, which slows its response even after model quality catches up.

**6. What would you 10x?**
Play #6 — close the shopping loop (§12), the play the dossier itself calls "the largest untapped revenue pool named in the sources." Meta AI already leads on shopping intent ("nobody else is bragging about shopping," §3) and business AIs already carry 10M weekly conversations, but the purchase itself doesn't happen inside the assistant (§7E, §12 gap #3). Closing that loop turns an existing intent signal plus an existing distribution surface (WhatsApp commerce rails) into a commerce take-rate across billions of conversations. The proof point is already specified: completed in-chat purchases in the LatAm/Indonesia SMB business-AI markets (§12).

**7. Design the GM's 1-page daily dashboard.**
Built around the actual North star (§8: time/engagement across the family converting to ad yield, with Meta AI sessions/user and glasses DAU as the leading indicators of the platform shift): family DAU trend, watched for outage/geopolitical noise rather than churn (§2, §11); impressions and price-per-ad moving together, not traded off (both up double digits in Q1, §2 — the actual ad-revenue engine); Meta AI sessions per user (double-digit % increase, §2); business-AI weekly conversations (10M and still unmonetized, §2, §3); AI-glasses DAU (tripled Y/Y, §2); and CapEx against free cash flow ($19.8B spent vs. $12.4B generated, §2, §9) — the number that tells the GM whether the platform bet is outrunning the cash it has to fund it.

**8. Pick the 1-2 metrics the whole company should focus on.**
Family engagement converting to ad yield — the actual North star (§8), and the one number that says the ~98%-of-revenue business is healthy regardless of what the platform bet does. Paired with Meta AI sessions per user and AI-glasses DAU (§8, §2) as the leading indicator of whether the platform-shift bet is working at all — since everything in §9 (CapEx, the model race, the glasses wager) is a bet on that number rising, not a guaranteed outcome.

**9. Build a me-too version in a few months — what's essential?**
Strip to the mechanism in §1: a ranked feed/recommendation engine as the engagement loop (the aha is content that feels made-for-you), a messaging layer for retention (the switching cost is the graph plus message history), and an ad auction where impressions × price is the only real revenue mechanism. Everything else — glasses, the Neural Band, business AIs, Reality Labs, the first-principles content model, an LLM-scale recommender — is real, but expansion, not load-bearing for a first cut. Cut hardware outright (a multi-year platform bet, not a months-long build, per §9); cut business-AI monetization (unproven, still free, per §2); cut the second-app strategy (Threads rides an existing graph — it doesn't need to exist for the mechanism to work).

**10. Pick one area, be the PM — walk through how you'd design it.**
The Creator segment (§7C) — job is "produce content + earn," friction is that 3D/immersive content is "really hard" to make, aha is "I prompted a world and it built it." Design: ship prompt-to-world creation (Play #5, §12) as the entry point — a text or voice prompt drives Horizon Studio's agentic assistant, which already stitches together existing generation tools (§3, §7C), to produce a shareable world directly in the feed. Attach commerce from day one rather than as a later add-on: affiliate/product-tagging is already in test (§7C), so creators get paid the moment they publish, which directly answers the segment's other named friction — monetization is thin. Success metric: worlds-created-per-day after the prompt-to-world beta (§12's own stated proof point).

**11. Cut the scope to 1/4 — what survives?**
The ranked-feed engagement engine (Passive Consumer, §7A) and the ad-AI monetization stack (Advertiser/SMB, §7D) survive — together they *are* the ~98%-of-revenue business, and §8 explicitly prioritizes "consumer-grade quality + distribution to billions and the ads flywheel that funds everything" over everything else. Cut the glasses/Reality Labs platform bet — RL revenue is shrinking (−2%, −12%, §2, §5) and the payoff is speculative, not table-stakes — and cut Creator commerce and the Connector presence layer as real but early, unproven monetization depth (§7B, §7C).

**12. Pitch an engineer who wants hard problems.**
The distillation-and-escalation inference system in §3: serving a trillion-parameter ad-ranking model "co-designed with the underlying silicon" to hold sub-second latency, while deciding per-request, in real time, how much compute to spend based on the predicted probability of a sale. Pair it with the claim in §3 and §5 that this is "the first recommendation model architecture that can scale with similar efficiency as LLMs" — a live systems-plus-ML problem operating at a scale almost nobody else runs, not the comparatively settled territory of general chatbot inference.

**13. Rebuild/remarket for a totally different, untargeted audience.**
Every segment in §7 — Passive Consumer, Connector, Creator, Advertiser/SMB, AI-First Shopper — is consumer- or SMB-facing; none is enterprise. Take Play #3 (§12, the autonomous ad agent built from value-optimization, video-gen, and incremental-attribution tooling, §1, §2) and redirect the same components at a buyer none of the five segments represent: enterprise IT, not SMB marketers. Package the ad-ranking engine and the distillation-escalation trick that already serves a trillion-parameter model in sub-second time (§3) as a licensed recommendation/ranking platform for retailers and marketplaces that aren't Meta advertisers today — same technology, an audience the dossier never targets.

**14. Remove the product constraint entirely — what else would you build?**
Drop "sells back into Meta's advertiser base" as a constraint entirely. License the ranking architecture itself — "the first recommendation model architecture that can scale with similar efficiency as LLMs" (§3, §5) plus the first-principles content-understanding model (§3) — as horizontal ranking infrastructure that any digital platform can build on, the way a cloud provider licenses compute. This still clears the right-to-win test (§12): it's tied to an asset only Meta has actually built and proven at 3.5B-user scale, not a speculative new capability.

Cite the $107B commitment increase [S1].

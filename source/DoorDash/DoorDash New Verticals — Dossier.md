# DoorDash New Verticals — Product Dossier
> DoorDash's push beyond restaurants — grocery, convenience, alcohol, retail, and its own DashMart dark stores (small delivery-only warehouses). The plan: take a food-delivery marketplace and make it the layer that runs all local commerce. The arc: from a money-loser to a second profit engine by H2 2026.
> **DASH** (parent) · ~$83.7B mkt cap (Q4'25) · PE(FWD) ~34 · Updated **2026-07-05** · Sources: **13** (see §15)
> Arc: **Facts → Insights → Differentiators → Plays.** Skim the bold headlines for the gist; drill for detail.
> *v1 — earnings-grounded + web research. New Verticals = grocery + convenience + alcohol + retail + DashMart. Earnings facts tagged [S1]/[S2]; web facts tagged [S3]+. Estimates explicitly labeled.*

## 1. Business anatomy — Acquire / Engage / Retain / Monetize

- **Acquire** — Cross-sell the installed base: turn 100M+ restaurant users into grocery/retail buyers (winning ~1 in 2 industry first-time grocery customers) [S1]. DashPass as the acquisition wrapper — the same subscription now covers non-restaurant categories [S2]. New merchant supply (Kroger, Family Dollar, +33 grocers in 2025) adds selection, which brings in customers on its own [S4].
- **Engage** — Core loop: order restaurants → notice grocery/convenience in the same app → repeat more often. Aha = "the delivery beat the drive to the store." **DoubleDash** hook: add items from a second nearby store with no extra delivery fee or minimum, which drives cross-category attach (grocery + a restaurant meal) [S7]. Frequency framing: 20-25 eating occasions/week, 100+/month — the app covers a single-digit fraction [S1][S2].
- **Retain** — DashPass: subscribers "retain more, order more, produce more gross profit dollars" [S2]. Switching cost = the physical catalog + reliable delivery (fewer substitutions). DashMart Fulfillment Services deepens retailer lock-in — DoorDash holds the inventory [S3]. Cohort data: existing cohorts spend a bigger share of their wallet over time [S2].
- **Monetize** — (1) Consumer delivery + service fees per order; (2) DashPass subscription (flat fee, more categories covered) [S2]; (3) **DoorDash Ads / retail media** — CPG Sponsored Products against grocery/convenience demand, including DashMart as owned ad space; grocery ads "earlier to maturation... lots of runway" [S2][S10]; (4) DashMart first-party retail margin (DoorDash as the retailer); (5) DashMart Fulfillment Services (fulfillment-as-a-service fees) [S3]. On purpose *not* over-reliant on ads for grocery profit [S1].

## 2. Numbers that signal depth

**Headline scale & penetration**
- ~30% of US monthly users order outside restaurants; goal 100% [S1][S2]. The arc: 20% (Dec'23) → 25% (Dec'24) → ~30% (2025-26) [S8].
- ~1 in 2 first-time industry grocery-delivery customers start on DoorDash [S1].
- Parent: 56M US monthly users / 100M+ annual customers (Q4'25) [S2].
- On Valentine's & Mother's Day 2025, >1M convenience/retail/alcohol orders *each day* [S7].

**Market arcs**
- DoorDash #1 US third-party grocery+retail by *order volume* since late 2025, lead extending (YipitData) [S1][S7][S12].
- Instacart grocery-delivery share by *dollars*: ~70% (2023) → 58% (2024) [S6]; a separate cut puts Instacart at 73% of 3P digital grocery sales, DoorDash ~14.9% of grocery intermediary sales [S6].

**Verticals & footprint**
- Categories: grocery, convenience, alcohol, retail; retail sub-segments include pets, flowers, health & beauty, sporting goods, home improvement (added 2024) [S8][S11].
- Alcohol: ~30,000 SKUs across 35 states + DC + Puerto Rico [S7].
- Kroger: ~2,700 stores live from Oct 1, 2025 — largest grocer on the platform [web search][S4].
- DashMart: ~100+ US locations (est. ~192 across US/Canada/Australia); ~2,000 SKUs each; 5,600-11,000 sq ft dark stores; delivery in as little as 30 min [S5].
- 2025 additions: Kroger, Family Dollar + 33 more grocery retailers [S4].

**Profitability (New Verticals P&L)**
- CFO: "we expect the overall new vertical portfolio to be gross profit positive in the second half [2026]... trending well" [S1][S2].
- Retail + grocery turn unit-economic positive in H2 2026 [S2].
- Drivers: "no one thing" — scale, density, logistics efficiency, larger baskets, cohorts maturing [S1][S2].

**Unit economics** *(cross-ref /follow-the-dollar)*
- Grocery basket > restaurant basket (more dollars per order); mid-week "quick runs" + weekend "stock-up" baskets [S2].
- Mature cohorts: basket size and order frequency both improving; each new cohort adopts faster [S1][S2].
- No segment-level revenue/GOV table disclosed in either transcript [S1][S2].

## 3. Wow Vault ★
*Non-obvious framings a sharp interviewer wouldn't already have. Ranked strongest first.*

**★ The competitor isn't Instacart — it's you walking into the store.**
- **Mechanism:** Management says the thing holding grocery back is neither demand nor selection but how good the experience is: "the online delivery experience is just not yet good enough compared to the offline experience." The root cause is physical: store inventory and payment systems "don't necessarily always talk to each other... it's really hard for them to know where things are" [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** Most candidates frame grocery delivery as a share fight with Instacart. The real ceiling is whether the customer just drives to the store instead, and whether items are out of stock. The market opens up the moment delivery beats the drive.
- **Deploy:** "How would you grow DoorDash grocery?" — recall hook: *"the competitor is your own two feet."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ A map of physical stores that "cannot be scraped."**
- **Mechanism:** DoorDash is building a digital catalog of the physical world — "where every banana sits, every ripe or unripe avocado, every size shoe... billions of items, tens of millions per city." Dashers collect it; it exists nowhere else online [S1][S2].
- **Why non-obvious:** As AI agents scrape and copy product catalogs, the asset that lasts is the ground-truth map of what's actually on the shelf — data an LLM can't crawl.
- **Deploy:** "What's DoorDash's moat against AI agents?" — recall hook: *"you can scrape a menu; you can't scrape which avocado is ripe."*
- **Source:** [S1][S2]

**★ "Best end-to-end beats best discovery" — winning the top of the funnel is worthless without delivery.**
- **Mechanism:** "What's the point of having the best discovery experience if we can't bring you that exact item, or it's out of stock?" History repeats: Google Food Ordering (2016) sent DoorDash several times its traffic for ~8 years, but those users came back at "a fraction" of the normal rate — because after checkout, things happen in the real world (a late driver, spoiled milk swapped for something else) [S1][S2].
- **Why non-obvious:** It flips the usual "distribution wins" belief. The value and the repeat orders sit in the messy last mile, not in discovery.
- **Deploy:** agentic-disintermediation and "who captures the value" prompts — recall hook: *"after checkout, the physical world happens."*
- **Source:** [S1][S2]

**★ About 1 in 2 first-time grocery-delivery customers in the whole industry start on DoorDash.**
- **Mechanism:** "We gained about 1 in every 2 new customers that comes into the industry for grocery delivery for the first time" [S1]. DoorDash turns its restaurant relationship into a grocery on-ramp.
- **Why non-obvious:** Instacart still has the bigger installed base (58–73% share depending on the cut), but DoorDash is winning the *flow* of new customers — the early sign of a share shift.
- **Deploy:** metrics prompts on leading vs. lagging indicators — recall hook: *"Instacart owns the stock; DoorDash owns the flow."*
- **Source:** [S1][S6]

**★ Profitable grocery without leaning on ads.**
- **Mechanism:** "We're not trying to rely on any one source of revenue like ads... we don't need to." Grocery reaches profit through a lower cost base and growing baskets over time, not an ad crutch [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** Instacart's grocery economics famously depend on retail ads. DoorDash says it can get to profit on delivery costs instead, which lasts longer and annoys shoppers less.
- **Deploy:** unit-economics and "is this business real" prompts — recall hook: *"profit from the cost structure, not the ad load."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ 30% of monthly users order outside restaurants today; the goal is 100%.**
- **Mechanism:** "About 30% of our monthly active users order from categories outside of the restaurant. We truly think that could be 100% over time" [S1][S2]. That share rose from 20% (Dec 2023) → 25% (Dec 2024) → ~30% (2025-26) [S8].
- **Why non-obvious:** Growth doesn't need new users — it needs the existing 100M+ restaurant users to add a category. Every restaurant customer is a future grocery customer.
- **Deploy:** growth-strategy and north-star prompts — recall hook: *"the market is already inside the app."*
- **Source:** [S1][S2][S8]

**★ #1 in grocery/retail by order count — but not by dollars.**
- **Mechanism:** DoorDash became the #1 US third-party grocery+retail marketplace by *order volume* (YipitData), passing rivals in late 2025 and extending the lead [S1][S7][S12]. But Instacart still leads by *sales dollars* — bigger baskets [S6].
- **Why non-obvious:** Who's "leader" depends on what you count. DoorDash wins on how often people order (its convenience-store roots — small, fast baskets); Instacart wins on basket size (big stock-up trips).
- **Deploy:** "are they winning?" — recall hook: *"orders vs. dollars — pick your metric, pick your winner."*
- **Source:** [S1][S6][S7][S12]

**★ DashMart Fulfillment Services: DoorDash rents its warehouse muscle back to retailers.**
- **Mechanism:** Retailers (CVS, Party City, Kroger to follow) put their inventory in DoorDash-run DashMart warehouses; DoorDash does the picking, packing, stock accuracy, and Dasher delivery — "perfect inventory accuracy at the lowest cost," same-hour or same-day. The pitch: no more substitutions or out-of-stocks [S2][S3].
- **Why non-obvious:** It flips the marketplace model — DoorDash goes from listing a retailer's inventory to *holding* it. This is the layer that fixes the "store systems don't talk to each other" problem.
- **Deploy:** "how does DoorDash fix substitutions?" and platform-vs-1P prompts — recall hook: *"own the shelf to nail the shelf."*
- **Source:** [S2][S3]

## 4. Reframes & mental models to borrow
*Restated so you can use them on any prompt.*

- **"The competitor is the offline experience."** Grocery delivery is won against the ease of walking into a store, not against other apps. → any growth/TAM prompt. [S1]
- **"A map of physical stores."** The moat is DoorDash's own ground-truth data on what's actually on the shelf, not its software. → moat / AI-defensibility prompts. [S1][S2]

- **"20-25 eating occasions a week, 100+ a month; we're a single-digit fraction."** Lots of room to grow — the app is nowhere near covering daily life. → north-star / frequency prompts. [S1][S2]
- **"Don't over-rely on any one cost or revenue driver."** Profit comes from many small gains, not one lever. → unit-economics prompts. [S1]

## 6. AI impact — cost-cutter or moat?

| AI vector | Cost-cutting / commoditizing | Moat-deepening |
|---|---|---|
| Agentic shopping assistants | Could turn the marketplace into a commodity logistics API | Management: agents are top-of-funnel "channel partners," can't deliver → DoorDash keeps the end-to-end job [S1][S2] |
| Physical-world catalog + AI | — | AI run on DoorDash's own un-scrapable stock data widens the lead [S1] |
| Merchant onboarding automation | Lowers the barrier for all platforms | Multi-turn models onboard merchants "with perfect quality every time" → faster supply growth [S1] |
| Ad optimization (Smart Campaigns) | Makes basic ad-buying a commodity | "Always ROI-positive" auto-campaigns lift retail-media earnings on owned demand [S2] |
| Autonomy (Dot / drones) | Self-driving tech eventually becomes a commodity | The value is orchestrating Dashers + self-driving vehicles, not the vehicle itself [S2] |

**Net read:** A tailwind for New Verticals. The delivery + own-catalog layer is exactly what AI agents can't copy, so AI mostly feeds the top of the funnel while DoorDash keeps the defensible last mile. The one real AI risk: a super-agent (Amazon/Google/OpenAI) bundles delivery through its *own* logistics and pushes DoorDash down to a cheap commodity carrier — the marketplace becomes a pipe.

## 7. Needs-based segments → problems → solutions
*Shreyas method — split by the JOB, not demographics. New Verticals users split on: how urgent the need is, how predictable the basket is, and how much the item's quality matters.*

**Segmentation basis:** the grocery/retail delivery job splits by *why* the order exists — an urgent gap-fill, a planned restock, a quality-critical fresh order, or a non-food errand I'd otherwise drive for. The same person sits in different segments on different days; that's the point.

**A. The "I ran out — now" gap-filler** — Job: refill one missing thing (diapers, milk, a phone charger) without leaving home; emotional: skip the store run that breaks up my task. Friction: minimum-order fees make a 1-item order feel silly; is it even in stock? Nudge: extrinsic (DoubleDash — piggyback on an order I'm already placing, no extra delivery fee). Aha: *"it arrived in 25 minutes, faster than I could've driven."* Today → gap: convenience/DashMart does this well (highest-frequency, most profitable vertical [S8]) but it's still buried under restaurants → **Play #2 (daily-runs home screen).**

**B. The weekly stock-up planner** — Job: restock the household for the week in one predictable basket; personal: hand off a chore I dislike. Friction: swaps on out-of-stock items break trust; a big basket means big risk if it's wrong; price vs. driving myself. Nudge: intrinsic (reliability builds a standing habit). Aha: *"nothing was substituted or missing — I stopped double-checking."* Today → gap: this is Instacart's stronghold; DoorDash lags on basket size [S6] → **Play #1 (inventory-accuracy guarantee) + Play #4 (recurring cart).**

**C. The fresh-quality-critical shopper** — Job: get produce/meat/perishables at the quality I'd pick myself. Friction: can't squeeze the avocado remotely; "ripe or unripe" is invisible online — the exact problem the physical catalog targets [S1]. Nudge: intrinsic (trust in the picker/quality). Aha: *"the avocados were actually ripe."* Today → gap: partly solved by DashMart owned inventory + catalog data; unsolved for third-party store shelves → **Play #1 + Play #5 (quality-scored picking).**

**D. The non-food errand-runner** — Job: get a same-day retail item (a gift, party supplies, a hardware part, OTC meds) without a special trip; social: look on top of it (last-minute gift saved). Friction: doesn't know DoorDash carries retail; assumes it's food-only. Nudge: extrinsic (occasion triggers — Valentine's/Mother's Day >1M retail orders/day [S7]). Aha: *"I got Party City supplies delivered in an hour."* Today → gap: awareness — retail breadth (flowers→footwear→home improvement) is under-marketed [S8] → **Play #3 (occasion-driven retail surfacing).**

**E. The retailer/CPG merchant (supply side)** — Job: reach delivery demand + fix out-of-stocks without building own logistics; social: compete with Amazon across channels. Friction: running your own inventory/delivery is hard; store systems don't sync stock [S1]. Nudge: extrinsic (revenue + new-customer visibility). Aha: *"DashMart fulfills it with perfect accuracy and I did nothing."* Today → gap: DashMart Fulfillment Services live with "a handful" of partners — not yet scaled [S1][S3] → **Play #6 (fulfillment-as-a-service scale-out).**

## 8. Strategy *(Shreyas Strategy Template)*
- **One-sentence strategy:** Turn DoorDash's restaurant installed base, Dasher density, and own physical-world catalog into the cheapest, most reliable delivery for *everything* local — making grocery/retail a second profit engine, not a grow-at-any-cost land grab.
- **Prioritize:** frequency + reliability (get the experience right before scaling) and getting existing users into more categories. **Don't over-serve:** maxing basket size or ad load — grocery profit has to come from the cost base, not from squeezing one lever [S1].
- **Pillars (moat → segment):** (1) physical-world catalog → fresh-quality shoppers (C); (2) Dasher density + DashMart → gap-fillers (A) & merchants (E); (3) DashPass wrapper → stock-up planners (B).
- **North star:** % of monthly users ordering outside restaurants (30% → 100%) [S1][S2] — paired with order frequency as the engagement metric.
- **Non-priorities (trade-offs):** not chasing dollars-per-order to match Instacart's basket; not making ads the main way grocery earns; not owning a self-driving fleet (orchestrate instead) [S1][S2].
- **Roadmap / metrics:** **Now** — inventory accuracy + DashMart Fulfillment (leading: substitution rate ↓; lagging: repeat-order rate). **Next** — grocery/retail gross-profit-positive H2'26 (leading: contribution margin per order; lagging: New Verticals gross profit). **Later** — 100% cross-category penetration + lower delivery cost from autonomy (leading: % of monthly users buying in multiple verticals; lagging: New Verticals EBITDA).

## 9. Contrarian bets & open tensions
- **Bet: grocery profit without ads.** Bear: Instacart shows grocery only works with heavy retail ads; DoorDash is skipping the highest-margin lever. Counter: the delivery-cost + basket-growth path lasts longer and treats shoppers better; ads are upside, not the plan [S1].
- **Bet: agents are partners, not disintermediators.** Bear: a dominant super-agent bundles its own delivery and turns DoorDash into a carrier. Counter: 8 years of Google Food Ordering shows top-of-funnel traffic without delivery doesn't retain [S1][S2].
- **Bet: own the shelf (DashMart 1P + Fulfillment Services).** Bear: capital-heavy dark stores + inventory risk drag on margins and pull focus off the asset-light marketplace. Counter: it's the only way to structurally kill substitutions — the #1 experience defect [S1][S3].
- **Best skeptic angle:** "leader by order volume" hides that Instacart still wins the dollars and the loyal stock-up shopper; DoorDash's grocery lead is thin, order-weighted, and unproven on margin. **Valuation tension:** the parent trades at ~34x forward PE on the promise that verticals turn gross-profit-positive H2'26 — a slip resets the multiple [S2].

## 10. Big Picture — Looks Wrong, Is Right

**A. Wise refusals**
- **Not maxing grocery basket size / not chasing Instacart's dollar-share** → the restraint is right: DoorDash's edge is *frequency* (its convenience roots), and forcing big baskets would fight its own strength. Win the daily habit and let basket grow as cohorts mature. [S1][S6]
- **Not leaning on retail-media ads to make grocery profitable** → skipping the easy lever keeps the experience clean ("much lower ad load than some other platforms") and forces a lasting fix in the cost base. [S1]
- **Not owning a self-driving fleet** → declining to bet on the vehicle; the value is orchestrating Dashers + self-driving vehicles. Owning fleets is capital that becomes a commodity. [S2]
- **Not fearing AI agents / not walling them off** → treating them as channel partners, not threats; delivery is the moat, so more top-of-funnel traffic is fine. [S1][S2]

**B. Counterintuitive moves**
- **Becoming a retailer (DashMart 1P) inside an asset-light marketplace** → looks like scope creep and inventory risk; is the only structural fix for substitutions/out-of-stocks and a new ad surface. [S3][S5]
- **Renting its delivery muscle back to retailers (Fulfillment Services)** → looks like arming would-be competitors (CVS, Kroger) to skip the marketplace; deepens their dependence instead — DoorDash holds their inventory and does their delivery. [S3]
- **Getting Dashers to catalog the physical world** → looks like unpaid busywork on a gig fleet; builds the un-scrapable data moat for the AI era. [S1][S2]
- **Charging the same DashPass fee while adding grocery/retail value** → looks like leaving money on the table; buys frequency + retention that produce more gross-profit dollars than a price hike would. [S2]

## 11. Mistakes & Mis-executions → Opportunities
- **Substitution/out-of-stock experience still broken outside DashMart** → *why* (root cause): third-party store inventory + payment systems "don't talk to each other," so DoorDash can't see real-time stock on partner shelves [S1] → *fix*: expand DashMart Fulfillment Services + real-time inventory hookups beyond the "handful" of current partners [S1][S3].
- **Retail breadth is under-discovered (awareness gap)** → *why*: users still think DoorDash is food-only; retail (flowers→footwear→home improvement) is buried under restaurant discovery [S8] → *fix*: occasion-driven retail surfacing + a separate non-food entry point (Play #3).
- **Basket size / dollar-share lags Instacart** → *why*: the product is built for small fast orders, not stock-up trips [S6] → *fix*: recurring/subscription carts + a planned-restock UX for Segment B.
- **Grocery has lost money for years** → *why*: too little density + high delivery cost in a low-margin category; profit only arriving H2'26 [S1][S2] → *fix*: the density/logistics path management is running; the risk is slipping the timeline. *(Management-acknowledged timeline, not a hidden error.)*
- **DashMart Fulfillment Services still sub-scale** → *why*: deliberate "get the experience right before we scale it" pacing [S1] → *opportunity*: the caution is defensible, but the land-grab window vs. Amazon/Instacart is open now. *(Debatable — restraint vs. speed.)* [S1][S3]

## 12. What's missing → Plays to run *(10× / 100× — hypotheses, not today)*

**Gaps:** (1) reliable fresh-quality picking outside owned stores; (2) a non-food/retail entry point most users never see; (3) a planned-restock UX for stock-up; (4) Fulfillment Services is sub-scale; (5) the physical catalog is a moat but not yet a feature customers can see.

- **Play #1 — Inventory-accuracy / no-substitution guarantee.** Move: show real-time in-stock confidence per item (from the physical catalog + DashMart data); guarantee it or credit the customer on substitutions. Gap: kills the #1 grocery defect. Why DoorDash: only it has the Dasher-collected ground-truth catalog [S1]. **10×** on stock-up trust (Segment B/C). Proof-point: A/B a "guaranteed in-stock" badge on DashMart SKUs, measure repeat-rate lift.
- **Play #2 — A daily-runs / "essentials" home surface.** Move: a dedicated convenience+grocery entry (not buried under restaurants) built for 1-3 item gap-fills. Gap: convenience is highest-frequency + most profitable yet under-surfaced [S8]. Why DoorDash: DashMart density enables sub-30-min [S5]. **10×** frequency for Segment A. Proof-point: home-screen module test, measure orders per user per week.
- **Play #3 — Occasion-driven retail surfacing.** Move: trigger retail (flowers, party, gifts, OTC) around calendar/occasion moments; push a "one-stop local errands" identity. Gap: retail awareness [S8]; occasion demand is proven (>1M retail orders on holidays [S7]). Why DoorDash: the breadth is already live (flowers→footwear→home improvement) [S8]. **10×** Segment D activation. Proof-point: occasion-triggered push campaign, measure new retail orderers.
- **Play #4 — Recurring / subscription cart.** Move: a standing weekly basket filled from cohort history, one-tap confirm. Gap: no planned-restock UX; basket lags Instacart [S6]. Why DoorDash: DashPass wrapper + cohort data [S2]. **10×** basket size + retention for Segment B. Proof-point: recurring-cart pilot with mature cohorts.
- **Play #5 — Quality-scored picking (the catalog, made visible).** Move: show ripeness/quality signals and let users set preferences ("ripe now" vs. "ripens later") using the physical catalog. Gap: fresh-quality trust (Segment C). Why DoorDash: the un-scrapable catalog is unique [S1]. **100×** differentiation vs. any rival on fresh. Proof-point: ripeness-preference toggle on produce in DashMart.
- **Play #6 — Fulfillment-as-a-service scale-out.** Move: onboard retailers to DashMart Fulfillment Services fast as the "Shopify of local delivery." Gap: sub-scale ("handful" of partners) [S1]. Why DoorDash: owned dark-store network + Dasher fleet [S3][S5]. **100×** — becomes the delivery backbone for local commerce (Segment E). Proof-point: convert 3-5 anchor retailers, measure inventory accuracy + cost per order.

**Small compounding wins:** substitution-credit auto-refunds · "reorder your usual" one-tap · Dasher pick-quality ratings · perishable-only fast lane · alcohol-attach at grocery checkout · pantry-staple price-match badges · in-app "did it arrive fresh?" feedback that feeds the catalog.

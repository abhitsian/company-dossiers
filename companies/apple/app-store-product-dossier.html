App Store — Product Dossier

Apple's own app store and payment system for 2.5B+ Apple devices — a curated store that is also a payments rail and, increasingly, an ad network. Moving from "30% toll booth" toward "commission-lite, ads-funded discovery engine" under regulatory pressure.

1Business anatomy — Acquire / Engage / Retain / Monetize▸
  • Acquire — Zero user CAC (cost to acquire a customer): the store is pre-installed on every one of 2.5B active devices and is the only sanctioned way to get apps on iOS (outside the EU). Developer-side acquisition via Developer Program ($99/yr), Developer Centers (US, China, India, Singapore, Berlin), ~20 Developer Academies. The supply side (developers) is acquired by reach; the demand side comes bundled with the hardware. S1S3
  • Engage — Core loop: search/browse → product page → one-tap install (stored Apple ID + payment) → in-app purchase. Editorial (Today tab, curation), charts, and Search Ads drive discovery. The aha is friction-free install-and-pay; >850M weekly users is the engagement KPI. S2S3
  • Retain — Switching cost is the whole iOS relationship: purchase history, subscriptions managed in one place, Family Sharing, Ask-to-Buy, refunds. For developers, retention is coerced (no alternative distribution ex-EU) but softening as external payments + EU marketplaces appear. Paid accounts + paid subs at all-time highs. S1S2
  • Monetize — Three lines: (1) commission on digital goods (30/15%, shrinking under regulation); (2) Developer Program fees ($99/yr flat); (3) advertising (Search Ads → multi-placement → Maps), the fastest-growing line and the strategic pivot. Price fences: SBP threshold ($1M), year-one vs year-two sub rate, EU CTC. S1S6S7
2Numbers that signal depth▸

Headline scale & product

  • $1.4T ecosystem billings 2025 (nearly tripled since 2019). Split: $1.1T physical goods/services · $149B digital goods · $151B in-app ads. S3
  • >850M weekly average App Store users, 175 countries/regions. S2S3
  • Developers earned >$550B since 2008. S2
  • 90%+ of billings are commission-free to Apple. S3
  • 40+ of top 100 apps have consumer-facing AI; those grew billings ~4x the rest. S3
  • Regional: US + Europe billings more than tripled in six years; China more than doubled. S3

Market arcs

  • App Store est. 63.2% of global gaming app revenue, 73.0% of non-gaming — a high-value minority of installs. S6
  • Contested: third-party data pegs App Store growth ~7% vs reported Services +14%. S2
  • Est. Apple advertising revenue ~$7.4B (2025), projected multi-billion annual adds by 2027 (analyst est., not Apple-disclosed). S6

Services context (App Store's parent line)

  • Services revenue $31B Q2 FY26 (+16%) and $30B Q1 FY26 (+14%), both all-time/record; Services GM 76.7% vs Products ~38–41%. GM = gross margin, the share of revenue left after direct costs. S1S2
  • All-time records within Services in advertising specifically, both quarters. S1S2

Unit economics

  • Standard commission 30%; 15% under Small Business Program (<$1M/yr proceeds, opt-in, manual). Subscriptions drop to 15% after year one. S7
  • EU alt terms: commission cut to 10–17%; Core Technology Commission 5% on promoted external digital sales; 10% on subs after year one when combined with SBP. S4
  • US (post-Epic): external payment links allowed; Apple's prior 27% external fee struck down, sent back for a lower cost-based fee (Supreme Court to hear, 2026). S5
  • On the ~$149B commissionable digital base, a blended effective take-rate (est. ~15–20% after SBP/sub-mix) implies roughly $25–30B gross App Store commission (estimate — Apple does not disclose). [est.]
3Wow Vault ★▸
The App Store's headline number ($1.4T ecosystem) is a magic trick — Apple never touches 90%+ of it.
  • Mechanism: Of $1.4T in 2025 billings, $1.1T is physical goods/services (Uber rides, Amazon orders, DoorDash) that carry zero commission; only the $149B digital-goods slice is commissionable, and even there the Small Business Program + subscriptions drop most to 15%. S3
  • Why non-obvious: People assume Apple takes 30% of $1.4T. It doesn't. The "$1.4T" is a regulatory shield — it lets Apple argue the store is mostly a free public good, which weakens the antitrust case.
  • Deploy: any "how does Apple monetize the App Store" or metrics question — recall hook: "$1.4T flows through, but the commissionable base is ~$150B."
  • Source: S3
Apple is quietly rebuilding App Store economics from a commission model to an ad model — because regulators can kill the first but not the second.
  • Mechanism: As Epic (US) and the DMA (EU) push commissions toward 0–17%, Apple is adding ads: search ads → multiple search placements (Mar 2026) → Maps ads (summer 2026). Ad business grew YoY both quarters; est. ~$7.4B ad revenue 2025. S1S6
  • Why non-obvious: The public reads "Apple loses App Store lawsuits" as margin loss. The real story: Apple is swapping a revenue line courts can cap (commission) for one they can't (ads against high-purchase-intent searches).
  • Deploy: strategy / "is the App Store's moat eroding" — recall hook: "They're trading the toll booth for a billboard."
  • Source: S1S6
App Store search is the highest-intent ad inventory on the internet, and Apple barely monetized it until now.
  • Mechanism: A user searching "expense tracker" in the App Store is one tap from installing and paying. Apple captured est. 63% of global gaming and 73% of non-gaming app revenue — a small but very high-value audience. Yet it ran a single ad slot for years. S6
  • Why non-obvious: Everyone compares App Store ads to Google Play's larger reach and misses that intent per impression, not reach, is the asset.
  • Deploy: product-sense on ad load / "where's the upside" — recall hook: "One tap from install-and-pay is the most valuable click Apple owns."
  • Source: S6
Contested growth: third-party data says App Store growth slowed to ~7% while Apple reports Services at +14–16% — and Apple refuses to break it out.
  • Mechanism: On the Q1 call, an analyst cited third-party data showing App Store growth ~7% vs the reported 14% Services line; the CFO would neither confirm nor deny, and fell back on "quarterly record." S2
  • Why non-obvious: The bundled Services line hides which sub-services are slowing. If the store's commission take is maturing, ads + cloud + services carry the growth.
  • Deploy: metrics / "what would you want to see broken out" — recall hook: "Services is a blended line hiding a possibly-flat App Store."
  • Source: S2
The App Store's real moat is not the store — it's Apple ID + on-file payment across 2.5B devices.
  • Mechanism: >850M weekly App Store users, transacting + paid accounts at all-time highs, all riding stored payment credentials and Apple's trust/privacy brand. A rival app store can copy the shelf; it can't copy the billing relationship Apple already has with 2.5B devices. S1S2S3
  • Why non-obvious: The DMA lets rivals distribute apps in the EU, but almost no one has switched — because distribution was never the hard part; the account + payment + trust layer is.
  • Deploy: moat / competition — recall hook: "Sideloading is legal in the EU and nobody left — the moat was billing, not shelf space."
  • Source: S1S3
AI apps are already the App Store's fastest-growing tenants — 40 of the top 100 apps, growing billings 4x the rest.
  • Mechanism: In 2025, >40 of the top 100 apps shipped consumer-facing AI, and those apps grew billings ~4x the other top-100 apps. S3
  • Why non-obvious: The App Store gets paid on the AI wave whether or not Apple Intelligence lands — every AI wrapper app pays through Apple's rails.
  • Deploy: AI-impact / "is AI a threat or tailwind to the store" — recall hook: "The store taxes the AI boom whether or not Apple wins AI."
  • Source: S3
Notarization is Apple's regulatory judo — it keeps a security chokepoint even after losing the distribution monopoly.
  • Mechanism: In the EU, apps distributed outside the App Store still pass Apple's Notarization (malware/privacy scan) and can incur a Core Technology Commission (5%). Apple gave up the shelf but kept the gate. S4
  • Why non-obvious: "Sideloading" sounds like Apple lost control; Notarization + CTC means Apple still inspects and can still charge on apps it doesn't even distribute.
  • Deploy: regulation / strategy — recall hook: "They lost the store but kept the customs checkpoint."
  • Source: S4
Ads let Apple claim the privacy high ground while building an ad network — the contradiction is the strategy.
  • Mechanism: Apple frames new App Store + Maps ads as helping "businesses of all sizes grow… while respecting people's fundamental right to privacy" — the same company that cut competitors' ad targeting with ATT is now expanding its own first-party ad inventory. S1
  • Why non-obvious: ATT (App Tracking Transparency) weakened third-party ads and pushed ad budgets toward first-party inventory Apple controls. The privacy stance is also a competitive moat for Apple's ad business.
  • Deploy: ethics / strategy / "second-order effects" — recall hook: "ATT was a privacy feature and an ad-network land grab."
  • Source: S1
4Reframes & mental models to borrow▸
  • "AI as an essential part of the experience, not a stand-alone feature." Apple's whole-product framing — apply to any "should we build an AI feature" prompt to argue for integration over a bolt-on chatbot. S1
  • "Businesses of all sizes grow via advertising while respecting privacy." Apple's framing of ad expansion as helping small businesses — useful when a monetization move needs a user/ecosystem-benefit story. S1
  • "90%+ commission-free." The store-as-public-utility framing — use when defending a marketplace against take-rate criticism: shrink the denominator that's actually taxed. S3
  • The installed base as "foundation for new growth." 2.5B devices framed not as a saturated market but as ready-made demand for the next service. S2
  • "Notarize, don't distribute." Keep the trust/security chokepoint even when forced to give up the distribution monopoly — a template for regulated gatekeepers. S4

Net read: Near-term tailwind — the App Store is the toll road for the AI-app gold rush and gets paid on the traffic. The one real AI risk to watch: a conversational/agentic layer that becomes the app front-end (find, install, and transact via an assistant), which would hollow out both the browse surface and the search-ad inventory Apple is betting its next revenue line on. Whoever owns that agent owns discovery — Apple's Siri/Apple Intelligence bet is a defense of the store's front door.

8Strategy (Shreyas Strategy Template)▸
  • One-sentence strategy: Be the trusted, default transaction layer for everything a person does on an Apple device — and as regulators cut the commission, replace that revenue with high-intent advertising and adjacent services, without giving up the trust/safety chokepoint.
  • Prioritize / Don't over-serve: Prioritize the billing + trust relationship with 2.5B devices and high-intent ad inventory. Don't over-serve the 30% commission fight — it's a losing legal battle; manage the decline, don't defend it.
  • Pillars (moat → segment): (1) Pre-provisioned billing → high-intent buyers + scaled devs; (2) Trust/Notarization → trust-first installers; (3) First-party intent ads → advertisers + indie discovery.
  • North star: Weekly transacting users on the store (proxy: the billing relationship is intact even as commission take falls). Secondary: ad revenue per high-intent session.
  • Non-priorities (trade-offs): Openness/sideloading (conceded only where legally forced); developer goodwill (traded for control); ad-free discovery (traded for ad revenue).
  • Roadmap / metrics:
    • Now — Expand search ads to multiple placements (Mar 2026) + Maps ads (summer 2026). Leading: ad impressions/session. Lagging: advertising revenue in Services. S1S6
    • Next — Absorb regulatory commission cuts (US external-fee remand, EU CTC) while holding billing share. Leading: % EU/US transactions still on Apple billing. Lagging: blended effective take-rate.
    • Later — Agentic/AI discovery front-end owned by Apple Intelligence. Leading: % installs via assistant. Lagging: store front-door retention vs third-party agents.
9Contrarian bets & open tensions▸
  • Bet: commission decline is fine because ads + services backfill it. Bear: ads on a smaller-reach store can't replace ~$25–30B of near-100%-margin commission fast enough; and more ad load degrades the trust that is the whole moat. Counter: intent-per-impression is the highest on the internet, ATT already pushed budgets to first-party inventory, and Services GM (76.7%) shows the mix can hold. S1S6
  • Bet: give up distribution, keep the gate (Notarization + CTC). Bear: regulators (EU) may strike the CTC as a disguised tax and force truly free distribution. Counter: near-zero EU switching proves distribution was never the moat — billing + trust are. S4
  • Bet: bundle the App Store inside "Services" and never break it out. Bear: the opacity invites regulatory + investor distrust; a slowing store hides in the blend. Counter: disclosure would arm both antitrust cases and competitors. S2
  • Best skeptic angle: the store's reported growth may already be ~7%, meaning the commission engine is mature and the ad pivot is a race against decline, not a growth story. S2
  • Valuation tension: Services carries Apple's multiple on 76.7% margins; if the App Store's commission line is capped by regulation, the premium leans entirely on ads + cloud + subscriptions executing.
10Big Picture — Looks Wrong, Is Right▸

A. Wise refusals

  • Won't break out App Store revenue → looks like hiding weakness; is right because a bundled Services line denies antitrust plaintiffs and rivals the exact take-rate figures that would anchor damages and "fair fee" arguments. S2
  • Won't cut commission voluntarily (only when forced) → looks stubborn/greedy; is right because every voluntary cut becomes the new legal floor everywhere — better to concede narrowly per-jurisdiction and keep the rate elsewhere. S4S5
  • Won't open iOS globally, only in the EU → looks like spiteful compliance; is right because it contains the precedent to one market and keeps the default-store economics for the other 90%+ of the base. S4

B. Counterintuitive moves

  • Expands ads while championing privacy (ATT) → looks hypocritical; serves the bigger play: suppress rivals' tracking-based ads, then grow first-party ad inventory that doesn't need cross-app tracking. S1
  • Lets developers link out to external payment (US) → looks like giving up the commission; serves the play of forcing courts to bless a lower cost-based fee Apple can then apply cleanly, turning a contempt loss into a defensible pricing model. S5
  • Keeps 90% of the ecosystem commission-free → looks like leaving money on the table; serves the play of shrinking the "monopoly rent" story to the ~$150B digital slice while banking the $1.1T physical-goods flow as free proof of ecosystem value. S3
11Mistakes & Mis-executions → Opportunities▸
  • App discovery is genuinely broken → why: Apple built the store for curation + charts + now ads, not for matching a stated need to the best app; search rewards keyword-stuffing and ad spend → fix: AI-native "describe what you need" discovery that ranks on fit, not bid — closes segment C's gap and defends against an external agent owning discovery. S3
  • Adversarial developer relationship → why: opaque review rejections, the 30% fight, and anti-steering rules bred a decade of litigation (Epic, Spotify, Match) → fix: reprice billing as a chosen premium service and publish review SLAs; turn coerced developers into willing customers before regulation forces it for free. S5
  • Late and reactive on external payments → why: Apple defended the closed model past the point of goodwill, earning a contempt finding for the 27% "work-around" → fix: lead with a transparent, cost-based fee globally instead of litigating each market to a standstill. S5
  • Ad load risks the trust moat → why: revenue pressure from commission decline pushes more ad slots into search (multi-placement Mar 2026), degrading result relevance → fix: cap ad density and label sponsored results clearly; protect the "first result actually solved it" experience that is the store's differentiator. S1S6
  • Curation doesn't scale to the AI-app flood → why: AI code-gen is multiplying low-quality and copycat apps faster than human editorial can vet → fix: automated provenance + trust-scoring (segment A). S3 (author judgment, not management-admitted)
12What's missing → Plays to run (10× / 100× — hypotheses, not today)▸

Gaps: discovery matches spend not need (C); indie devs can't be found (B); scam/copycat apps erode trust (A); billing is a mandatory tax not a chosen service (D); ad inventory is single-surface (E).

  • Play — Agentic "describe your need" install. Move: an Apple Intelligence layer where a user states a job ("split bills with roommates") and gets the best-fit app installed in one confirm. Gap: broken search (C). Why Apple: owns the on-device assistant + install + payment + the only trusted app catalog. 100× on discovery quality; defends the store's front door from third-party agents. Proof-point: a "Siri, find me an app that…" beta ranked on fit, ad-free. S3
  • Play — Small-dev discovery lane. Move: reserve intent-matched organic placement for sub-$1M developers (the SBP cohort) that ads can't buy. Gap: indie invisibility (B). Why Apple: it already identifies SBP developers and owns the ranking. 10× for the long tail; a goodwill + antitrust-defense asset. Proof-point: a "New & Noteworthy for your search" shelf. S7
  • Play — Unbundle billing as a premium, not a toll. Move: let developers pick Apple billing (for global tax/refund/subscription ops) vs external, and price it as a fair service. Gap: coerced-take resentment (D). Why Apple: the billing infra genuinely removes ops burden across 175 countries. 10× on developer NPS; turns litigation into a product. Proof-point: opt-in Apple-billing tier with published value (fraud, tax, refunds). S5
  • Play — Full-funnel Apple Ads network. Move: extend high-intent ads from App Store search to Maps, News, and Apple Intelligence answers as one privacy-first network. Gap: single-surface ads (E). Why Apple: first-party intent signal + ATT-protected position. 10× the ad line, backfilling commission decline. Proof-point: Maps ads (US/Canada, summer 2026) as the beachhead. S1S6
  • Play — AI trust-scoring + provenance labels. Move: automated malware/scam/copycat + "made-with-real-data" provenance badges on every product page. Gap: trust under the AI-app flood (A). Why Apple: Notarization pipeline already scans every app. 10× on trust; hardens the safety moat. Proof-point: a visible "Apple-verified" trust score. S3S4

Small compounding wins: clearer sponsored-result labeling; faster/transparent review SLAs; one-tap subscription-price-change alerts; refund self-service; per-app "why am I seeing this" for ads; a "declining charges" pre-purchase summary.

13Interview arsenal▸
  • [Product design] "Redesign App Store discovery." → Segment C + Play 1: agentic describe-your-need install; rank on fit, protect against ad-load degrading relevance (§7, §12).
  • [Product sense] "Is the App Store's moat eroding?" → No — distribution was never the moat; billing + trust on 2.5B devices is, proven by near-zero EU switching after sideloading (§1, §5).
  • [Strategy] "How should Apple respond to losing commission cases?" → Manage the commission decline, move revenue to first-party high-intent ads, keep the Notarization gate (§8, §10).
  • [Metrics] "What one metric would you break out and why?" → Weekly transacting users + effective take-rate; the blended Services line hides a possibly-~7% store (§1, §3).
  • [Product sense] "Should Apple put more ads in the App Store?" → Yes on inventory, but cap density — the trust that makes the ad intent valuable is the same trust ad-load destroys (§6, §11).
  • [Estimation] "Estimate App Store commission revenue." → ~$149B commissionable digital base × ~15–20% blended take ≈ $25–30B gross (label estimate; $1.4T headline is 90%+ untaxed) (§3).
  • [Execution] "You run App Store developer relations — first move?" → Reprice billing as a chosen premium service + publish review SLAs before regulation forces it free (§11, §12).
  • [Behavioral / judgment] "Defend Apple expanding ads after ATT." → Name the tension honestly: it's a privacy stance and an ad-network land grab; second-order strategy, not pure hypocrisy (§1, §10).

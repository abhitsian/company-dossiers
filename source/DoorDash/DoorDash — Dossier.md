## 1. Business anatomy — Acquire / Engage / Retain / Monetize

**Acquire** — how new users first find and start using the product. For DoorDash, the main engine is cross-category conversion of its own existing restaurant users into grocery and retail, with rented channels (search partners, in-store foot traffic) as secondary top-of-funnel.
- New-vertical grocery as an acquisition engine: ~1 in 2 new-to-industry grocery customers captured. [S1] Getting existing restaurant MAUs to cross into new categories (the 30%→100% crossover) is the cheapest acquisition — same user, new job. [S1][S2]
- Channel partners (Google historically; AI agents prospectively) drive "a small percentage" of traffic — treated as rentable top-of-funnel, not owned. [S1][S2]
- In-store business (Dash Forward, Sept '25) sends foot traffic to restaurants via DashPass access/value — an acquire/re-engage hybrid. [S2]

**Engage** — what makes users come back and how often. DoorDash's loop is order → fulfill end-to-end → expand into adjacent categories, with fulfillment reliability (the right item, in stock, on time) as the hook rather than discovery.
- Core loop: order → fulfill end-to-end → expand into adjacent categories (restaurant → grocery → retail → DashMart). Frequency thesis: mid-week "quick runs" + weekend "stock-up" baskets; each successive cohort adopts faster; existing cohorts grow share of wallet. [S2]
- The aha = the exact item arrives, in-stock, on time — fulfillment quality is the engagement hook, not discovery. [S1]
- Surface-by-surface: DashPass (membership), Ads (merchant-facing), DoorDash for Business (corporate: DashPass + Meal Manager + catering + group ordering, newly launched workplace catering). [S1]

**Retain** — what keeps users from leaving. DoorDash relies on DashPass membership plus the accumulated switching cost of a multi-category habit, backed by fulfillment infrastructure that cuts the substitutions/out-of-stocks that normally drive churn.
- DashPass is the retention spine ("critical," core since 2017/2018): record subscribers in Q4 AND FY25; growth from both sign-ups and retention, across DoorDash, Deliveroo, and Wolt (record quarter). [S1][S2]
- Switching cost = accumulated multi-category habit + membership value expansion (more categories at "effectively the same fee"). [S2]
- Boring plumbing that compounds: the proprietary physical-world catalog (fewer substitutions/out-of-stocks via DashMart Fulfillment Services) directly attacks the #1 grocery churn driver. [S1]

**Monetize** — how the company turns usage into revenue. DoorDash's mix is marketplace commissions (the primary line), DashPass subscriptions, merchant/CPG advertising, and a corporate line, kept below rivals on ad load by design.
- **Marketplace commissions** (restaurant + new verticals) — primary line, GOV-driven; no take-rate disclosed.
- **DashPass / memberships** — subscription; monetized through retention + frequency + gross-profit dollars, not price hikes ("effectively the same fee" while adding categories). [S2]
- **Ads** — "record high," fastest-growing; SMBs, large restaurant/retail advertisers, and CPG; Smart Campaigns (an agent that buys ROI-positive campaigns for merchants); Symbiosis-driven advertiser + spend growth. Expanding into off-site/syndicated ads, in-store, and buying on behalf of advertisers off DoorDash. Reinvested, not margined. [S1][S2]
- **DoorDash for Business** — corporate/catering line, "off to a very great start." [S1]
- Price fences: lower ad load than rivals (a deliberate consumer-experience ceiling on monetization). [S1]

## 3. Wow Vault ★

**★ The moat is a proprietary catalog of the physical world that AI can't scrape, not logistics**
- **Mechanism:** DoorDash is building "a digital catalog of structured information for the physical world" — where every banana sits, whether an avocado is ripe or unripe, every shoe size — "billions of items, tens of millions per city," collected by Dashers, "unique and proprietary to DoorDash, which we don't have to share with anybody" and "cannot be scraped." [S1] This data "does not exist anywhere on the internet." [S2]
- **Why non-obvious:** Most people assume a delivery marketplace's moat is courier density or restaurant supply. The durable asset is a real-world inventory graph built one Dasher trip at a time — a data moat, and the one thing an AI can't copy by crawling.
- **Deploy:** Any "will AI commoditize marketplaces?" or moat question — recall hook: *"you can scrape a menu; you can't scrape where the ripe avocados are."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ Owning the top of the funnel is overrated — controlling search never won the customer**
- **Mechanism:** Google Food Ordering (launched ~2016) ran ~8 years and drove "multiple-fold" of DoorDash's traffic, but "the retention of that traffic was a fraction" — customers "effectively moved all of their shopping experiences to DoorDash." Same pattern: Amazon owned product search by end of the 2010s. [S1][S2] Reason: "after a checkout, things can happen in the physical world" — a late driver, a missing item, spoiled-milk substitution. [S2]
- **Why non-obvious:** The common fear is that a personal AI agent sitting above the marketplace turns DoorDash into a commodity logistics API. Management's answer is from data, not hope: a bigger top-of-funnel competitor already ran the experiment for 8 years and lost the customer at fulfillment.
- **Deploy:** Agentic-commerce / platform-risk questions — recall hook: *"Google out-trafficked them for 8 years and still lost the customer — the physical world is where retention is won."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ "Best end-to-end beats best discovery"**
- **Mechanism:** "What's the point of having the best discovery experience if we can't bring you that exact item or... it's out of stock." [S1] Winning the upper funnel is worthless without fulfillment; agents "will be partners of ours... drive a small percentage of our traffic, and a lot of that will be a choice that we'll have." [S1]
- **Why non-obvious:** Goes against the usual PM instinct that discovery and search are the high ground. DoorDash argues the last mile is the defensible layer and discovery is the part anyone can copy.
- **Deploy:** "Where does value accrue in a stack?" strategy questions — recall hook: *"discovery is rentable; fulfillment is owned."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ In grocery, the real competitor is you walking into the store — not Instacart or Amazon**
- **Mechanism:** The binding constraint in grocery "isn't demand or selection — it's that the online delivery experience is just not yet good enough compared to the offline experience." Root cause: store inventory and payment systems "don't necessarily always talk to each other... it's really hard for them to know where things are." [S1]
- **Why non-obvious:** Moves the grocery TAM battle away from competitor share and toward closing an experience gap versus the physical store — which is why the DashMart Fulfillment Services inventory layer matters more than another ads lever.
- **Deploy:** Grocery strategy / "how would you grow new verticals" — recall hook: *"they're not fighting Instacart, they're fighting the parking lot."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ Autonomy is an orchestration layer, not a vehicle bet**
- **Mechanism:** "The autonomous delivery platform is probably the most valuable part of what we're building." The value is orchestrating handoffs between Dashers, ground AVs (DoorDash Dot, purpose-built for suburbs), and drones (rural) — not owning one fleet. [S2] Steph Curry analogy: shipping a demo vehicle vs. one that "can really operate at scale under any condition" — "I can shoot a 3-point shot and so can Steph Curry." [S1]
- **Why non-obvious:** Most read DoorDash autonomy as a Waymo-style capex race. They position as the demand + routing brain that stays fleet-agnostic — the layer that survives whichever AV hardware wins.
- **Deploy:** "Should X build or buy AVs?" / platform-vs-asset questions — recall hook: *"anyone can ship a demo AV; the moat is orchestrating a mixed fleet at scale."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ 2/3 of DoorDash's code is now AI-written — but they separate ship-speed from customer outcomes**
- **Mechanism:** "Well north of half... probably closer to 2/3 of our code is written by AI today," with "90-plus percent daily active usage across all engineers." [S1][S2] Caveat: shipping features faster is not the same as delivering customer outcomes faster — "the customer holds us to a higher bar." [S1]
- **Why non-obvious:** The interesting part is the discipline, not the stat. Management won't let a productivity metric stand in for a value metric — a sharp answer for eval/metrics questions.
- **Deploy:** AI-productivity or "vanity metric" questions — recall hook: *"2/3 AI-written code, and they still won't claim customer outcomes got 2/3 faster."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ Agent reliability crossed a threshold in the last ~6–8 months, changing the build calculus**
- **Mechanism:** "Even perhaps 6 to 7 or 6 to 8 months ago... you would need to build a lot of backup or redundant systems." Now multi-turn reasoning models finish repetitive merchant-onboarding tasks "with perfect quality every single time." [S1]
- **Why non-obvious:** A concrete, dated inflection in agent reliability from an operator — evidence that "agents are production-ready for narrow repetitive tasks" is now true, not aspirational.
- **Deploy:** "When is an agent good enough to ship?" / AI-PRD questions — recall hook: *"the redundancy tax on agents disappeared in the last two quarters."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ Grocery delivery "should be as large, if not larger, than restaurant delivery" — and only ~30% of MAUs have crossed over**
- **Mechanism:** "About 30% of our monthly active users order from categories outside the restaurant. We truly think that could be 100% over time." [S1] DoorDash "gained about 1 in every 2 new customers that comes into the industry for grocery delivery for the first time," and became volume-share leader in fall, "continued to extend." [S1]
- **Why non-obvious:** The headline (a restaurant company) hides that its second act is a same-or-larger TAM where it already leads new-customer capture at a 50% rate — a growth story hiding inside a maturing one.
- **Deploy:** TAM / growth-durability / "is the core saturating?" questions — recall hook: *"70% of their own users haven't bought groceries from them yet — that's the runway."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ Best-of-breed memberships: the streaming analogy for why multi-subscription isn't a threat**
- **Mechanism:** Memberships "only matter if they are best-of-breed experiences"; like streaming, consumers hold multiple. TAM: "20 to 25 occasions for eating alone every single week... over 100 every single month," and DoorDash is "a tiny fraction" — "a single-digit percentage of what DashPass could actually achieve." [S1][S2]
- **Why non-obvious:** Answers the "subscription fatigue" bear case — the frequency ceiling sits so far above current usage that the constraint is experience quality, not wallet share.
- **Deploy:** Subscription strategy / "is DashPass saturated?" — recall hook: *"a couple orders a week against 100+ eating occasions a month — they're rounding-error penetrated."*
- **Source:** [S1], [S2]

**★ An ad dollar is treated like a unit-economics improvement, not margin**
- **Mechanism:** CFO: "an ad dollar is very similar to how we think about improvement we generate from unit economics" — reinvested to grow long-term free cash flow, not dropped to margin. Ads run at "much lower ad load than some other platforms" by design. [S1]
- **Why non-obvious:** Most people model delivery ads as a high-margin add-on. DoorDash puts ad profit back into price and experience rather than showing it as margin — a choice that explains why margins look flatter than the ads growth would imply.
- **Deploy:** "Why isn't the ads business showing up in margin?" / monetization questions — recall hook: *"they put ad profit back into unit economics on purpose."*
- **Source:** [S1]

## 4. Reframes & mental models to borrow

- **"Best-of-breed or it doesn't matter."** A subscription only earns a slot if it's category-best — people hold multiple subscriptions (streaming), so the fight is quality, not exclusivity. → subscription/retention questions. [S1]
- **"Channel partners, not disintermediators."** Treat any threatening top-of-funnel player (Google, an AI agent) as a new "Google" that rents you traffic, not one that owns your customer — because retention is won after checkout, in the physical world. → platform-risk / agentic-commerce. [S2]
- **"The competitor is the offline experience."** In grocery, measure against walking into the store, not against other apps. → new-market entry / growth. [S1]
- **"Operating system for local commerce."** Position as the software + warehousing + lowest-cost-delivery layer that lets SMBs be omnichannel against "1 or 2 large behemoths" (Amazon). → strategy / competitive-framing. [S2]
- **"Profit dollars, not incremental margins."** "We're not... operating the business towards incremental margins" — optimize gross-profit dollars, not margin percentage. → metrics / "what's the right north star." [S2]
- **"Don't over-rely on any one revenue or cost driver."** Grocery gets profitable through cost structure + basket growth, "without any unnatural overreliance on any one" lever like ads. → durability / quality-of-earnings. [S1]
- **"Steph Curry vs. the demo shot."** Anyone can ship a demo (AV, feature); the moat is doing it "at scale under any condition." → build-vs-hype / execution. [S1]
- **"Ship-speed ≠ outcome-speed."** AI writing 2/3 of code speeds up output, not necessarily customer value — "the customer holds us to a higher bar." → AI metrics / evals. [S1]

## 6. AI impact — deflationary or moat?

| AI vector | Deflationary | Moat-deepening |
|---|---|---|
| Personal AI agents at top-of-funnel | Could take over discovery, shrink the marketplace to a logistics API | Top-of-funnel historically loses the customer post-checkout; agents become rentable channel partners [S1][S2] |
| Physical-world catalog + AI | — | AI applied to proprietary uncrawlable data widens the lead in agentic commerce [S1] |
| Coding agents (2/3 of code) | Rivals also get faster shipping → table stakes | Faster iteration on a proprietary data/logistics base; outcome-bar discipline [S1][S2] |
| Agent reliability for ops (onboarding) | Lowers cost for all platforms | "Perfect quality every time" on repetitive merchant tasks removes the redundancy tax at DoorDash's scale [S1] |
| Autonomy (AVs/drones) | Capex race anyone can enter; demo AVs are cheap | Orchestration + demand + real-world data = the layer that survives whichever hardware wins [S1][S2] |
| Ads + agentic buying | Third-party agents may capture some ad intent ("ask them") | "May increase the opportunities"; lower ad load + ROAS focus defensible [S1] |

**Net read:** Net **tailwind** for DoorDash. Its defensible assets (proprietary physical-world catalog, end-to-end fulfillment, mixed-fleet orchestration) are exactly the ones AI *can't* commoditize by crawling, while AI cheapens the parts it doesn't depend on (discovery, code). The one real AI risk to watch: a personal agent that captures enough repeat intent to erode DoorDash's *choice* over which traffic it accepts. If agents move from top-of-funnel referrers to habitual re-order engines, the "we choose the partnership" leverage weakens. [S1][S2]

## 7. Needs-based segments → problems → solutions

**Segmentation basis:** cut on the job-to-be-done and the friction that blocks it (immediacy, provisioning-for-others, in-stock reliability, spend-control), not demographics. DoorDash's own framing — "20–25 eating occasions alone," mid-week quick-runs vs weekend stock-ups, 30% cross-category — is need-shaped, so segments follow the occasion, not the person. Each passes the 5-point test (consistent need · product-specific · targetable via order data · prioritizable · winnable given the fulfillment moat).

**A. The solo-immediacy eater** — Job (functional): feed myself now, low effort; (emotional): no decision fatigue. Friction: single-order occasions are frequent (100+/month) but DoorDash captures a single-digit % of them. Nudge (intrinsic): reliably good, fast, best-of-breed. Aha: *"the exact thing I wanted, hot, in 25 minutes."* Today → gap: large frequency headroom, competing against cooking/walking out. → **Play: frequency-capture (§12).** [S1][S2]

**B. The weekly grocery provisioner** — Job (functional): stock the household without a store trip; (personal): reclaim time. Friction: online grocery "not yet good enough vs offline" — substitutions, out-of-stocks, inventory systems that don't talk. Nudge (intrinsic): trust that what I ordered arrives. Aha: *"zero substitutions, everything in the cart."* Today → gap: only ~30% of MAUs cross over; the experience gap is the blocker. → **Play: inventory-truth layer / DashMart Fulfillment Services (§12).** [S1]

**C. The mid-week quick-runner** — Job (functional): a few missing items fast; (social): don't derail the day. Friction: small basket, delivery cost sensitivity. Nudge (extrinsic): DashPass makes small runs free. Aha: *"three items, no delivery fee, 15 minutes."* Today → gap: DashPass frequency at "a couple times a week" vs far higher occasion count. → **Play: DashPass occasion-expansion (§12).** [S2]

**D. The workplace / corporate buyer** — Job (functional): feed a team, expense-clean; (social): host well. Friction: catering is "exponentially difficult" (kitchen capacity, staffing, logistics for large groups). Nudge (extrinsic): Meal Manager + group ordering + corporate DashPass. Aha: *"catered lunch for 40, one invoice, on time."* Today → gap: workplace catering just launched, "very great start" but nascent. → **Play: own the B2B catering fulfillment stack (§12).** [S1]

**E. The SMB / merchant advertiser** — Job (functional): grow orders profitably without an ads team; (emotional): confidence it's ROI-positive. Friction: SMBs historically can't run campaigns. Nudge (extrinsic): Smart Campaigns auto-buys "always ROI-positive" spend. Aha: *"I did nothing and my orders went up."* Today → gap: restaurant-side maturing, grocery/retail/CPG ads early. → **Play: self-driving ads for retail/CPG (§12).** [S1][S2]

## 8. Strategy *(Shreyas Strategy Template)*

- **One-sentence strategy:** Own the end-to-end fulfillment of local commerce — build a proprietary structured catalog of the physical world and a fleet-agnostic delivery orchestration layer that no top-of-funnel player (search or AI agent) can cut out.
- **Prioritize / Don't over-serve:** Prioritize fulfillment quality, category crossover (30%→100%), and profit-dollar production. Don't over-serve discovery/upper-funnel (rentable) or chase incremental margin-% at the expense of profit dollars. [S1][S2]
- **Pillars (moat → segment):** (1) Physical-world catalog → grocery provisioner; (2) End-to-end last mile → all segments; (3) DashPass habit → frequency segments; (4) Ads flywheel → merchants; (5) Autonomy orchestration → cost structure. [S1][S2]
- **North star:** Gross-profit dollars produced across the marketplace (explicitly not incremental margin %). [S2]
- **Non-priorities (trade-offs):** Not maximizing ad load (caps ad revenue for consumer trust); not owning a single AV fleet; not guiding to incremental margins; accepting triple-stack drag through '26 to gain long-run velocity. [S1][S2]
- **Roadmap / metrics:** Now — replatform to one stack, gas-rewards, ads expansion (leading: advertiser count / lagging: ad revenue mix). Next — new verticals gross-profit-positive 2H26, international ex-ROO contribution-positive (leading: basket size & cohort frequency / lagging: NV gross profit). Later — autonomy at scale, 30%→100% crossover, agent partnerships (leading: % MAUs ordering outside restaurant / lagging: GOV per MAU). [S1][S2]

## 10. Big Picture — Looks Wrong, Is Right

**A. Wise refusals** — what critics say DoorDash *should* do, but not doing it is the right call.
- **Don't max out ad load** → critics want more ad revenue now; keeping "much lower ad load than some other platforms" protects the consumer trust that drives the frequency (100+ occasions/month) where the real money is. Ad load is a demand tax you don't want to pay. [S1]
- **Don't chase incremental margins** → analysts pushed on margin expansion; "we're not operating the business towards incremental margins." Optimizing margin-% would starve the reinvestment (catalog, autonomy, replatform) that compounds profit *dollars* — the bigger pool. [S2]
- **Don't build one AV fleet** → critics want a Waymo-style hardware bet; owning a fleet locks you to one technology in an unsettled market. Staying fleet-agnostic keeps the valuable orchestration layer and defers capex until the hardware winner is clear. [S1][S2]
- **Don't drop ad profit to margin** → treating an ad dollar "like a unit-economics improvement" and reinvesting it looks like leaving money on the table; it buys price/experience that widens the fulfillment moat and long-run FCF. [S1]
- **Don't over-rely on ads for grocery profitability** → the easy path is to ads-subsidize grocery to profit; refusing it ("we don't need to") means grocery profits from a structurally lower cost base, which is durable rather than promotional. [S1]

**B. Counterintuitive moves** — what DoorDash *does* that looks like a mistake but serves a bigger play.
- **Run three redundant tech stacks and eat "ship every feature 3 times"** → looks like operational bloat; the replatform buys one global velocity engine and the redundancy cost "comes off" after '26 — a temporary tax for permanent speed. [S1][S2]
- **Send Dashers to catalog bananas and avocado ripeness** → looks like a low-value data-entry chore on top of delivery; it builds the uncrawlable physical-world catalog that is the durable moat in agentic commerce. [S1]
- **Let AI write 2/3 of the code but refuse to claim faster customer outcomes** → looks like under-selling a headline metric; the discipline protects credibility and keeps the org honest that output ≠ value. [S1]
- **Spend "several hundred million" reinvesting into the platform during quarters that miss revenue** → looks like poor cost control into a slowdown; it front-runs the infrastructure (domain mapping "done," production traffic flowing) that the next decade of local commerce runs on. [S1]
- **Time-shift H1 investments to H2 to fund gas rewards, keeping FY EBITDA flat** → looks like financial engineering; it holds the annual commitment while absorbing a $50M/qtr demand program — disciplined, not evasive. [S1]

## 13. Interview arsenal

**A. The Deconstruct Set**

**1. Why this company?**
DoorDash inverts the standard marketplace assumption that owning search/discovery is the high ground: the moat is a proprietary, Dasher-collected catalog of the physical world (item-level stock, ripeness, sizes) that can't be scraped, paired with end-to-end fulfillment that a bigger top-of-funnel player (Google Food Ordering, 8 years, "multiple-fold" more traffic) already tried and failed to convert into retention (§3 Wow Vault, §8 Positioning). The bull case reduces to one checkable question: can the 30%→100% grocery/retail crossover and the new-vertical gross-profit-positive guide (2H26) land before the triple-stack replatform tax and two straight quarters of revenue misses erode confidence in the PE 34.3 multiple (§2, §9).

**2. What do you like about it?**
The physical-world catalog moat — "you can scrape a menu; you can't scrape where the ripe avocados are" — because it's built one Dasher trip at a time and widens with every order, not something a well-funded rival can buy its way into overnight (§3 Wow Vault, §5 Moats). The Google Food Ordering precedent, which turns "agents will disintermediate marketplaces" from a fear into a tested case: 8 years of a bigger top-of-funnel player driving traffic, and the customer still moved downstream to whoever owned fulfillment (§3). And the autonomy stance — fleet-agnostic orchestration across Dashers, ground AVs, and drones instead of a single-vehicle capex bet — which keeps DoorDash the layer that survives regardless of which AV hardware wins (§3, §5 Moats).

**3. What don't you like about it?**
Three DoorDash brands (DoorDash, Wolt, Deliveroo) still run on separate stacks, so every feature ships three times, and the drag bleeds into early 2027 (§5 Weaknesses). Online grocery is explicitly behind the offline store — the root cause is that store inventory and payment systems "don't necessarily always talk to each other," which is why substitutions and out-of-stocks persist (§5, §11). Revenue missed consensus two quarters running (Q1 by $114.8M, Q4 by $31M), and a chunk of demand currently runs on a $50M/quarter gas-rewards program with no committed extension (§5, §11). Management also declined to quantify autonomy's addressable share, which leaves conviction without a number for anyone trying to model it (§5, §9).

**4. How would you fix that?**
The triple-stack drag has a stated fix already in motion — finish the single global stack in 2026, which removes both the P&L cost and the feature-velocity tax letting Uber press in Northern Europe (§11). The grocery gap is a speed problem, not a strategy problem: DashMart Fulfillment Services already closes the substitution/OOS gap where it's live (Kroger, CVS), so the highest-leverage move is racing that inventory-truth layer to every partner instead of adding more selection or ads on top of a broken base (§11, §12). The margin-deceleration risk from DashPass mix is best offset by leaning harder into the levers that produce gross-profit dollars without diluting per-order restaurant margin — new-vertical profit and the self-driving ads expansion into retail/CPG (§11, §12).

**5. How would you compete with it?**
Not on last-mile logistics — that's the moat DoorDash has already stress-tested against a bigger, better-funded top-of-funnel competitor (Google) and won by default because the customer is retained at fulfillment, not at discovery (§3, §8). Two real openings instead. First, grocery: DoorDash's own dossier names the online-grocery experience gap as the binding constraint, and DashMart Fulfillment Services is live with only "a handful" of partners plus Kroger and CVS named — a grocery-native player (Instacart, or a regional grocer's own app) that solves real-time inventory truth through direct POS integration, rather than Dasher-observed stock, can out-execute on the exact job DoorDash admits it hasn't solved yet (§5, §7B, §11). Second, corporate catering: DoorDash for Business just launched workplace catering and calls the underlying job — feeding a large group reliably — "exponentially difficult"; a specialist built around kitchen-capacity and staffing logistics (not a logistics company bolting on catering) has a real window before DoorDash's supply-side tooling matures (§7D, §11). A third, more structural attack: press hardest in the two years the triple-stack replatform is still running, since DoorDash is paying a "ship every feature 3 times" tax across DoorDash, Wolt, and Deliveroo through '26 — Northern Europe under Uber is the named pressure point (§5 Weaknesses, §9).

**6. What would you 10x?**
The inventory-truth layer — scaling DashMart Fulfillment Services from a handful of partners to the full grocery footprint (§12). It's the highest-leverage play because it attacks the one gap management itself names as the binding constraint on the entire grocery vertical, it's already proven at two named partners (Kroger, CVS), and grocery is a same-or-larger TAM than restaurant delivery with only ~30% of DoorDash's own MAUs currently crossed over (§3, §7B, §12). Fixing substitutions and out-of-stocks doesn't just improve grocery — it's the unlock for the 30%→100% crossover the whole new-vertical growth story depends on.

**7. Design the GM's 1-page daily dashboard.**
Anchor it on gross-profit dollars produced, the explicit north star, tracked daily against guide rather than margin percentage (§8). Below that: % of MAUs ordering outside restaurant (the crossover metric currently ~30% against a stated 100% ambition), DashPass net adds and retention across all three brands, substitution/out-of-stock rate at DashMart Fulfillment Services partners (the leading indicator for the grocery experience gap), advertiser count and ad spend growth (Symbiosis: doubled advertisers, tripled spend), and gas-rewards spend against incremental GOV lift, since that's the one line testing whether current demand is durable or subsidized (§2, §5, §12).

**8. Pick the 1-2 metrics the whole company should focus on.**
Gross-profit dollars, not incremental margin percentage — management states this explicitly as the north star, because optimizing margin-% would cap the profit pool that funds the catalog, autonomy, and replatform investments (§8). Paired with % of MAUs ordering outside restaurant: it's the single number that tells you whether the second act (grocery/retail, a same-or-larger TAM) is actually converting the base DoorDash already owns, rather than depending on new customer acquisition (§2, §3, §7B).

**9. Build a me-too version in a few months — what's essential?**
Strip §1 to the mechanism: a two-sided marketplace matching local merchant supply to on-demand consumer requests, a courier network for last-mile fulfillment, and a paid membership that waives delivery fees to build frequency. That's the whole loop — order, fulfill, retain. Everything else is expansion, not foundation: the proprietary physical-world catalog, autonomy orchestration, Ads/Symbiosis, DoorDash for Business/catering, and the international triple-stack are all real moats DoorDash spent years building, but none of them are load-bearing for a first cut of the core loop (§1).

**10. Pick one area, be the PM — walk through how you'd design it.**
The weekly grocery provisioner (§7B) — explicitly the largest gap in the dossier. Job: stock the household without a store trip. Friction: the online experience isn't yet good enough versus offline, because store inventory and payment systems don't talk to each other, so orders arrive with substitutions or missing items. Aha: everything in the cart arrives, no substitutions. Design: extend DashMart Fulfillment Services partner-by-partner with direct POS/inventory integration (not Dasher-observed stock) starting from the two live partners, Kroger and CVS; surface real-time confidence per item in the cart before checkout rather than after; and let a customer pre-set substitution preferences per category so the rare miss is a choice, not a surprise. Success metric: substitution/OOS rate trending toward zero on integrated partners, and lift in the 30% crossover rate among MAUs who complete one clean grocery order (§7B, §11, §12).

**11. Cut the scope to 1/4 — what survives?**
The solo-immediacy eater (A) and the weekly grocery provisioner (B) survive: A is the existing frequency base, B is the strategically prioritized, same-or-larger TAM that §8 explicitly names as a priority (§7). The mid-week quick-runner (C) folds into A/B rather than staying separate — it's really a DashPass frequency-expansion problem on top of the same fulfillment core, not a distinct build. The workplace/corporate buyer (D) and the SMB/merchant advertiser (E) get cut: catering is nascent and "exponentially difficult" with no proven supply-side tooling yet, and the ads layer is a monetization overlay on fulfillment that DoorDash can turn back on once the core loop is solid, not something that needs to exist for the core loop to work (§7, §8, §11).

**12. Pitch an engineer who wants hard problems.**
The autonomy orchestration layer (§3, §5 Moats): routing across three fundamentally different delivery modes — human Dashers, ground autonomous vehicles purpose-built for suburbs (DoorDash Dot), and drones for rural — in real time, without owning any single fleet, and without the luxury of controlling the hardware's reliability curve the way a single-vehicle operator would. Management's own framing is the pitch: shipping one demo AV that works under ideal conditions is easy ("I can shoot a 3-point shot"); building the routing brain that operates a heterogeneous, third-party fleet "at scale under any condition" is the actual hard problem, and it's a problem nobody else is running at this mix of scale and vehicle diversity (§3).

**13. Rebuild/remarket for a totally different, untargeted audience.**
Take the "operating system for local commerce" positioning — software, warehousing, and lowest-cost delivery for SMBs to compete with Amazon (§4, §8) — and drop the word "commerce" entirely. The same Dasher network, routing logic, and DashMart Fulfillment infrastructure DoorDash built for restaurants and grocery is a general local-delivery utility: pharmacy and specimen delivery for healthcare, same-day parts delivery for home-services trades, light industrial and B2B last-mile that has nothing to do with a consumer ordering dinner. None of DoorDash's current segments (§7) are built for this audience, but the underlying asset — dense, fleet-agnostic local delivery capacity — doesn't care what's in the bag.

**14. Remove the product constraint entirely — what else would you build?**
Drop "must move a physical item" too. The proprietary catalog — a live, Dasher-collected record of what's actually true in the physical world right now (shelf stock, item condition, ripeness, location) — is valuable independent of delivery. License it as a horizontal ground-truth data layer: to insurers needing real-world condition data, to other retailers needing inventory signal they can't otherwise get, to AI agents that need grounding in physical reality instead of scraped, stale web data. This still passes the right-to-win test from §12 — it's the one asset in the dossier explicitly described as impossible to scrape and "unique and proprietary to DoorDash," built continuously by a Dasher network no one else has, so a rival can't replicate the data by trying harder, only by building an equivalent physical presence over years (§3, §12).

# Uber Advertising — Product Dossier
> An ad business built on Uber's Rides + Eats data — who buys what, and where they are — selling ad space inside the app people already use to move and eat. Arc: from a $500M sponsored-listings add-on (2022) to a >$2B run-rate ad platform that can trace an ad view all the way to a purchase, racing DoorDash and Instacart for grocery and retail ad money.
> **Product of UBER** · parent mkt cap ~$151.5B · FWD P/E ~24.6 · Updated **2026-07-04** · Sources: **9** (see §15)
> **v1 — earnings-grounded + web research**
> Arc: **Facts → Insights → Differentiators → Plays.** Skim bold headlines for the gist; drill for detail.

## 1. Wow Vault ★
*What makes an interviewer lean in on Uber Advertising specifically.*

**★ The ad business grows ~2.5x faster than Uber's bookings and is almost all profit**
- **Mechanism:** Ads run-rate crossed **>$2B annualized by Q4 2025, +>50% Y/Y** [S5], on top of a bookings base growing ~21% [S1]. Each extra ad dollar costs Uber almost nothing to earn — the screens (rider phones, the Eats feed) and the audience already exist for the core business. That is why a line that is under 2% of bookings moves earnings "twice as fast as bookings" for the parent [S1].
- **Why non-obvious:** People think of Uber as rides + food. The highest-margin dollar in the P&L is an ad, not a trip.
- **Deploy:** "Where's the margin coming from?" / monetization prompts — recall hook: *"the fastest-growing, highest-margin line at Uber isn't a ride — it's an ad against a ride."*
- **Source:** [S5][S1]

**★ Penetration blew past the "2% of delivery bookings" ceiling — and management now calls 2% too low**
- **Mechanism:** Uber long guided ads to ~2% of Delivery gross bookings. Q4 FY2025: "penetration in delivery now exceeding your prior target of 2%… the opportunity size is potentially much larger" [S2]. For scale: 2% of a ~$90B+ annual Delivery bookings base ≈ the ~$2B run-rate — and where Amazon and Instacart land on ad-heavy screens (high-single-digit take) is several times higher.
- **Why non-obvious:** The stated 2% target was a floor dressed up as a ceiling. The number to watch is the *gap to Amazon-class penetration*, not today's 2%.
- **Deploy:** metrics / TAM sizing — recall hook: *"2% was never the ceiling; it was the parking brake."*
- **Source:** [S2]

**★ Enterprise ad revenue now out-grows SMB — the runway is CPG catching up, not SMB maxing out**
- **Mechanism:** SMB (restaurant) ad penetration is "a lot higher than 2%"; enterprise/CPG Y/Y growth is "now outpacing SMBs by a lot more" [S2]. Restaurants adopted first — feed placement decides how many orders they get. Big CPG and retail brands are the later, larger group just ramping up. (CPG = packaged-goods brands like Coke or Pepsi.)
- **Why non-obvious:** Most assume the SMB long tail is the growth story. Here the *enterprise* brand budget is the under-used asset.
- **Deploy:** segmentation / GTM prompts — recall hook: *"restaurants taught Uber ads; Coca-Cola is the second, bigger student."*
- **Source:** [S2]

**★ 60% of Uber Eats sessions start with no idea what to order — that's the whole thesis**
- **Mechanism:** "60% of Uber Eats users open the app without knowing what they want to order" [S3]. An undecided user is the most valuable person in advertising: in-market, in-app, ready to be swayed, with a card on file one tap away.
- **Why non-obvious:** It makes Eats a *demand-creation* surface, not just a way to place an order — closer to a grocery-store endcap than to a Google search.
- **Deploy:** product-sense / "why is this ad inventory valuable" — recall hook: *"the most valuable ad is shown to a hungry person who hasn't decided yet."*
- **Source:** [S3]

**★ Uber can trace an ad from the view all the way to the purchase — including buying in a physical store**
- **Mechanism:** Uber sees the ad view, the tap, the order, *and* the delivery — and through Destination Offers, a purchase in a physical store the rider is heading toward [S4][S5]. Lumen study: Uber ads earned **6.6x higher attention** than online video/social/display; JourneyTV +11% attention; post-checkout ads +40% performance [S5].
- **Why non-obvious:** What pulls CPG budgets is the proof, not the ad space. A brand can show that a ride-screen ad led to a burrito or an in-store visit.
- **Deploy:** "what's defensible here" / measurement — recall hook: *"Uber closes the loop from the ad to the aisle."*
- **Source:** [S4][S5]

**★ Uber handed its own long tail to a competitor's ad stack (Instacart Carrot Ads)**
- **Mechanism:** Apr 15, 2025 — Uber added **Instacart's Carrot Ads** so CPG advertisers can build sponsored-product campaigns on Uber Eats grocery/retail through Instacart Ads Manager [S6]. Carrot already supports 7,000+ brands and 220+ retail banners [S6][S8].
- **Why non-obvious:** Buy-vs-build flipped — instead of building its own self-serve CPG tool, Uber rented one from the top grocery ad player to reach "brands of all sizes" fast [S6].
- **Deploy:** build-vs-buy / partnership strategy — recall hook: *"Uber borrowed Instacart's ad engine to sell against Instacart."*
- **Source:** [S6]

**★ Advertising quietly pays for Uber One's economics**
- **Mechanism:** Uber One (>50M members, >50% of bookings [S1]) cuts fees and gives back credits (hotels 10%, grocery no-fee >$60 [S1][S2]). Ad margin is a profit pool that helps fund those member give-backs without hurting group margin.
- **Why non-obvious:** The membership give-aways look expensive until you see the ad dollar covering them.
- **Deploy:** business-model / cross-subsidy — recall hook: *"the ad line pays for the membership perks."*
- **Source:** [S1][S2] (linkage is my read, not stated)

## 2. Reframes & mental models to borrow
- **"On the way to somewhere."** Every Uber user is moving with a known start, destination, and arrival time — you target on *motion and intent*, not cookies. Use on: "why is this ad inventory different from Meta's?" [S4]
- **"Undecided in-market demand."** 60% of Eats opens are undecided [S3] — the ad *makes* the choice instead of catching one that already exists. Use on: product-sense, "what job does the ad do?"
- **"Close the loop to the aisle."** Tracking runs ad view → order → in-store purchase [S4]. Use on: measurement / defensibility.
- **"2% was a floor, not a ceiling."** [S2] Use on: TAM sizing / how to read a penetration target.
- **"Restaurants first, CPG second."** Adoption follows who lives on the feed (SMB) before who has the big budget (enterprise) [S2]. Use on: GTM sequencing.
- **"AI dissolves the fixed UI."** Dara: the UI becomes "whatever the user wants" [S1] — for ads, that means built-in, personalized placements instead of fixed slots. Use on: future-of-ad-formats.

## 4. Business anatomy — Acquire / Engage / Retain / Monetize

**Acquire (advertisers)**
- **Enterprise/CPG** direct sales — global head Kristi Argyilan, vertical GMs for ride app / grocery / Eats-restaurant / CPG after the Grether reorg [S3]. Clients: Coca-Cola, Pepsi, McDonald's, Molson Coors, Ulta [S5].
- **SMB long tail (restaurants)** — self-serve sponsored listings inside the merchant tools; restaurants adopt first because feed rank = orders [S2].
- **CPG long tail** — through the **Instacart Carrot Ads** integration (Apr 2025), reaching "brands of all sizes" without Uber building its own self-serve CPG tool [S6].
- **Agencies** — "embracing us more" for signals they can't get elsewhere (event attendance, airport traffic) [S3]; 20+ measurement partners lower the trust barrier [S5].
- **Offsite Ads** — buy attention off-platform (Google Shopping, Meta) and send it back to Uber apps [S4].

**Engage (the ad surfaces)**
- **Rides:** Journey Ads (pre/in-trip), Homescreen Ride Offers, Ride Offers on Journey, Sponsored Upgrades (brand-paid Comfort/Black), JourneyTV in-car video, **Destination Offers** (buy in-store on the way) [S4][S5].
- **Eats:** Sponsored Listings / Sponsored Products (auction, merchant- or brand-paid), **Brand Takeover** (full-screen on open), **Item Showcase** (shoppable carousel, add-to-cart), **Offers Spotlight**, **Post-Checkout Ads** (the leaned-back, "hungry for more" moment) [S4].
- **The aha:** an ad shown to an *undecided, in-market* user (60% of Eats opens [S3]) converts far above interruptive display.

**Retain (advertisers)**
- End-to-end tracking → provable ROAS keeps budgets renewing [S5]. Example: Pizza Hut Space Invaders game on Rides drove "very high" ROAS [S3].
- A growing roster of measurement partners (Comscore, VideoAmp…) makes Uber a hard-to-drop line item in media plans [S5].

**Monetize**
- **Take-rate model:** ad revenue as a % of gross bookings — the "2%+" Delivery line, with SMB well above and enterprise/grocery/retail below (runway) [S2].
- **Formats price by attention/position:** premium takeovers and JourneyTV cost more than feed listings [S4].
- **Expansion vectors:** grocery + retail + mobility ad products are early — same audience, new ad space, more take [S2].

## 6. AI impact — deflationary or moat?

| AI vector | Deflationary | Moat-deepening |
|---|---|---|
| Personalized/dynamic UI ("UI is whatever the user wants" [S1]) | | Built-in, per-user ad placement instead of fixed slots — Uber's intent data feeds it |
| Destination prediction (75% of rides predicted [S1]) | | Ad targeting on *predicted* intent, not typed queries |
| Agentic commerce (Eats in ChatGPT; OpenAI/OpenTable ties [S5]) | Risk: the agent hides the app and removes the ad surface | If Uber owns the fulfillment API, it can put sponsored results into agent answers |
| Generative creative | Makes ad-making cheap; lowers the SMB barrier | More advertisers → more auction competition on Uber inventory |
| Measurement/attribution ML | | Sharper end-to-end ROAS = stickier budgets |

**Net read:** Net **tailwind** — Uber's edge is its own intent data + end-to-end measurement, exactly the assets AI makes stronger rather than cheap. **The one real AI risk:** the agent cutting Uber out — if a personal agent (ChatGPT/Gemini) becomes the ordering layer, the ad surface (the feed, the takeover) may never be shown to a human, shrinking display inventory to one sponsored line in an agent's answer. Dara's counter is the metasearch analogy (value went to Expedia/Booking, not the front ends [S1]) + owning the fulfillment API.

## 7. Needs-based segments → problems → solutions
**Segmentation basis:** the *advertiser's* job-to-be-done — what business outcome the ad buys — not company size or industry. (Uber Advertising's customer is the brand; the rider/eater is the audience whose intent is the product.) Axes: outcome sought (awareness ↔ conversion ↔ real-world visit) × certainty of demand (undecided ↔ decided) × build capacity (has ads team ↔ none).

**A. The performance CPG brand — "prove the ad sold a unit."**
- **Job:** functional — drive measurable added sales; personal — defend the ad budget to a CFO with ROAS.
- **Friction:** delivery ad measurement is fragmented; grocery/retail products still early on Uber [S2].
- **Nudge:** extrinsic — end-to-end tracking + 20+ measurement partners [S5]; the Carrot Ads bridge for familiar tooling [S6].
- **Aha:** *"I can see this ride-screen ad drove a checkout — and an in-store visit."*
- **Today → gap:** Uber has the signal but thinner self-serve than Amazon → gap in campaign tooling depth → **Play #1 (native self-serve CPG suite).**

**B. The undecided-demand restaurant/QSR — "catch the hungry, undecided opener."**
- **Job:** functional — win the order before the user decides; emotional — beat the restaurant next door in the feed.
- **Friction:** the feed is crowded; organic rank keeps slipping as more merchants join.
- **Nudge:** intrinsic — placement converts directly (Sponsored Listings, Item Showcase add-to-cart) [S4].
- **Aha:** *"60% of these people hadn't decided — and I was the option they saw first."* [S3]
- **Today → gap:** SMB penetration already high [S2]; gap is *creative quality* for small merchants → **Play #4 (generative creative for SMB).**

**C. The real-world-intent brand (retail, auto, entertainment) — "reach someone on the way to my category."**
- **Job:** functional — shape an offline purchase before it happens; social — show up at a relevant moment (airport, event).
- **Friction:** hard to link a digital ad to a physical visit anywhere else.
- **Nudge:** extrinsic — Destination Offers redeemable in-store; targeting on trip destination/airport traffic [S4][S3].
- **Aha:** *"I reached her in the car, on the way to the mall, with an offer she used at the counter."*
- **Today → gap:** limited to where in-store redemption is wired up → **Play #2 (offline-redemption network).**

**D. The awareness/brand advertiser — "own a full-screen, high-attention moment."**
- **Job:** functional — be remembered; emotional — a premium, brand-safe canvas.
- **Friction:** digital video attention is low; social feeds are cluttered.
- **Nudge:** extrinsic — Brand Takeover, JourneyTV (+11% attention), 6.6x attention vs. digital (Lumen) [S4][S5].
- **Aha:** *"A captive rider watched my full ad — attention I can't buy on social."*
- **Today → gap:** in-car video capped at ~50k screens [S5] → **Play #3 (scale JourneyTV / rider-screen CTV).**

**E. The self-serve long-tail (SMB CPG, local) — "spend $200 and see it work."**
- **Job:** functional — cheap, fast, provable local reach.
- **Friction:** no ads team, no agency; Uber historically sold to enterprise.
- **Nudge:** extrinsic — Instacart Ads Manager access via Carrot Ads for "brands of all sizes" [S6].
- **Aha:** *"I ran a sponsored-product campaign myself in an afternoon."*
- **Today → gap:** depends on a partner's UI, not Uber's own → **Play #1.**

## 8. Strategy *(Shreyas Strategy Template)*
- **One-sentence strategy:** Monetize the highest-intent audience on the internet — people moving toward a purchase — by turning Uber's first-party rides + Eats data into an ad network that traces the ad to the purchase, near-pure-margin dollars added on top of existing bookings.
- **Prioritize:** advertisers who want *provable conversion* against *undecided in-market* demand (CPG, QSR, retail). **Don't over-serve:** pure top-of-funnel brand budgets that don't care about end-to-end measurement — that's Meta/CTV's game.
- **Pillars (moat → segment):** (1) first-party intent + location → performance CPG [A]; (2) undecided-demand feed → restaurants [B]; (3) ad-to-aisle tracking → real-world-intent brands [C]; (4) high-attention captive canvas → brand advertisers [D].
- **North star:** ad revenue as % of gross bookings (penetration), moving from ~2% toward Amazon-class multiples [S2].
- **Non-priorities (trade-offs):** protect rider/eater trust over near-term ad load; do not build a full owned media network beyond Uber surfaces; rent (Carrot Ads) rather than build the CPG long-tail tool for now [S6].
- **Roadmap / metrics:**
  - **Now** — grow Eats/grocery penetration + measurement. *Leading:* # active advertisers, auction density. *Lagging:* ad % of Delivery GBs [S2].
  - **Next** — scale Mobility/JourneyTV and Destination Offers; native self-serve. *Leading:* JourneyTV screen count, self-serve advertiser adds. *Lagging:* ad % of Mobility GBs.
  - **Later** — agentic-commerce sponsored placements; offsite network. *Leading:* sponsored-result impressions in agent/offsite channels. *Lagging:* total ads run-rate.

## 9. Contrarian bets & open tensions
- **Bet: renting Instacart's Carrot Ads beats building self-serve.** *Bear:* Uber hands the CPG tooling relationship — and data — to a competitor, and it's hard to win the long tail back later. *Counter:* speed-to-revenue and reach ("brands of all sizes" now) beat owning a tool Uber would take years to match [S6].
- **Bet: more ads won't hurt the core experience.** *Bear:* the surface is valuable *because* it's trusted; every takeover and JourneyTV spot taxes that trust. *Counter:* undecided-demand users treat relevant offers as useful, not as interruption; penetration is still low [S2][S3].
- **Bet: agentic commerce grows the pie, it doesn't kill it.** *Bear:* if ChatGPT orders the food, no human sees the ad — display inventory collapses. *Counter:* the metasearch analogy — value pools to the fulfillment platform, and Uber can put sponsored results into the agent [S1][S5].
- **Valuation tension:** the ad business alone is probably worth a double-digit revenue multiple, but it sits inside a bookings-multiple parent (~24x FWD P/E [S1]) — a sum-of-parts skeptic would say ads is under-credited; a bear would say it's still <2% of the delivery pool [S2].
- **Best skeptic angle:** "You're 4th to a party (Amazon, Instacart, DoorDash) with rented tools and a departed founding GM — what's durable?" Answer with the intent + ad-to-aisle assets none of them own [S4][S5].

## 10. Big Picture — Looks Wrong, Is Right

**A. Wise refusals**
- **Not building a full self-serve CPG ad platform (renting Carrot Ads instead)** → the restraint is right: reaching "brands of all sizes" *now* via Instacart's 7,000-brand engine beats a multi-year build; Uber keeps focus on its scarce asset (intent data), not on rebuilding an ad-ops UI [S6].
- **Holding ad penetration below the obvious maximum** → capping ad load on purpose protects the trust that makes an *undecided-demand* surface worth more than a cluttered one; the ">2%, target too low" framing shows patience, not a missed number [S2].
- **Not chasing pure brand/awareness budgets head-on with Meta/CTV** → skipping the low-differentiation fight; Uber wins where *measurement + intent* matter, not where reach is a commodity [S5].

**B. Counterintuitive moves**
- **Partnering with Instacart — a direct grocery-media rival — on ad tooling** → the bigger play: use a competitor's rails to speed up Uber's own CPG monetization while Uber's real moat (rides + location intent) stays its own [S6].
- **Putting ads *inside a ride* (JourneyTV, Journey Ads, Space Invaders game)** → looks like cluttering a transport product; it actually monetizes the one place with a captive, high-attention audience digital can't buy (6.6x attention) [S4][S5].
- **Post-Checkout Ads — advertising *after* the user already bought** → looks like a wasted moment; it's the leaned-back, satisfied, "hungry for more" state with the highest receptivity and the card already on file [S4].

[S6]
- **Grocery, retail, and mobility ad products left "nascent"** while the biggest budgets (CPG, retail media) sit right there [S2] → *why:* focus and headcount went to the proven Eats-restaurant feed first → *opportunity:* the fastest-growing budget pool in advertising is retail media; Uber is under-indexed on its own largest surface. [S2]
- **Founding-GM churn (Grether → PayPal Ads) mid-scale-up, plus a vertical reorg** [S3] → *why:* talent competition + reorganizing around verticals before the platform matured → *opportunity:* Argyilan's retail-media background is a chance to reset toward one coherent, measurement-led platform instead of selling format by format. [S3]
- **In-car video capped at ~50,000 screens** [S5] → *why:* hardware/rollout cost and driver friction → *opportunity:* the rider *phone* screen as the CTV canvas (no hardware) scales JourneyTV-class attention to the whole base. [S5]
- **Ads absent from Q1 2026 investor narrative** [S1] → *why:* still small vs. Mobility/AV headlines → *opportunity (comms):* an under-told story; a clearer stand-alone disclosure would let the market credit the highest-margin line. [S1]

## 12. What's missing → Plays to run *(10× / 100× — hypotheses, not today)*

**Gaps:** no native self-serve CPG tool (rented from Instacart); grocery/retail ads early; in-car video hardware-capped; no offline-redemption network at scale; agentic-commerce sponsorship unbuilt.

- **Play #1 — Native self-serve ad platform ("Uber Marketing Manager" for real).** *Closes:* CPG/SMB tooling gap [S6]. *Why Uber can:* it owns the intent data and the auction; only the UI is rented. *10×* on advertiser count. *Proof:* ship a self-serve sponsored-products beta in 2 US metros, measure advertiser adds vs. Carrot-routed.
- **Play #2 — Destination-Offer offline-redemption network.** *Closes:* the "reach on the way, convert in-store" gap [S4]. *Why Uber can:* it uniquely knows destination + arrival time; no other platform sees the drive-to-store. *100×* on a new (offline retail) budget pool. *Proof:* one QSR/retail chain, ad-to-purchase lift study on in-store redemptions from ride-screen offers.
- **Play #3 — Rider-phone CTV (scale JourneyTV without hardware).** *Closes:* the 50k-screen cap [S5]. *Why Uber can:* every rider already holds a screen and a known trip length. *10×* on high-attention video inventory. *Proof:* opt-in phone Journey Ads with attention measurement vs. tablet baseline.
- **Play #4 — Generative creative for SMB.** *Closes:* small-merchant creative-quality gap [B]. *Why Uber can:* it has the menu/photo data and the placements. *10×* on SMB campaign quality/conversion. *Proof:* auto-generate Item Showcase creative for 500 restaurants, measure CTR lift.
- **Play #5 — Sponsored results in agentic commerce.** *Closes:* the risk of the agent cutting Uber out [S1][S5]. *Why Uber can:* it owns fulfillment; the agent needs Uber's supply. *100×* by defending the ad model in the agent era. *Proof:* a sponsored-restaurant slot in the Eats-in-ChatGPT flow with disclosed labeling.

**Small compounding wins:** more measurement partners (each opens an agency); Offers Spotlight expansion; more post-checkout formats; more auction competition from more SMB advertisers; airport/event targeting packages [S3][S5].

# DashPass — Product Dossier

> DoorDash's $9.99/mo membership that drops the delivery fee to $0 and lowers the service fee on eligible orders — the retention-and-frequency engine under a marketplace that only earns when you order again.
> **DASH** (parent) · membership is a sub-product, not separately priced-out · Updated **2026-07-05** · Sources: **10** (see §15)
> **v1 — earnings-grounded + web research.** Arc: **Facts → Insights → Differentiators → Plays.** Skim the bold headlines for the gist; drill for detail.
> *Interview-prep tuned (product sense / strategy / design / metrics). Every fact `[S#]`-tagged; estimates labeled "(est.)".*

## 1. Business anatomy — Acquire / Engage / Retain / Monetize

- **Acquire** — 30-day free trial as the front door; **partner-seeded distribution** is the standout channel: Chase Sapphire Reserve/Preferred bundle DashPass free, T-Mobile gives 12 months free, RBC/other card tie-ins [S7][S10]. These bring in high-intent, high-LTV members at near-zero DoorDash CAC and set up a payment relationship. Also: student/SNAP-EBT discounted tiers to widen the top of funnel [S8][S9]. Family Sharing adds a second member per household for free [S7].
- **Engage** — the core loop is *fee relief made visible*: the green DashPass badge + "$0 delivery fee over $12" on every eligible merchant, so the member feels the saving at each checkout [S3][S6]. Aha = the first 2–3 orders where saved fees exceed the $9.99. Engagement widens surface-by-surface: restaurants → grocery/DashMart → retail → pickup (5% back) → in-store (Dash Forward, Sept'25) → Lyft/streaming, each adding occasions to the member's week [S1][S2].
- **Retain** — the top retention driver in the business; DoorDash calls DashPass "critical" and the "core driver of consumer relationship since 2017/2018" [S2]. The switching cost isn't contractual (cancel anytime) — it's *sunk habit + accumulated benefit breadth*: once grocery, pickup credits, Lyft, and streaming all run through the membership, the member has several reasons not to cancel. Retention improving Y/Y even at a mature base [S1].
- **Monetize** — three stacked lines: (1) **membership fee** ($9.99/$96, counted inside GOV) [S4]; (2) **higher marketplace take from member frequency** — the fee is loss-leader-ish, the order stream is the payoff [S2]; (3) **ad revenue on the traffic the member generates** — Ads "at a record high," CPG/SMB/retail advertisers, and members are the highest-frequency eyeballs [S1]. Price fences: subtotal minimums ($12/$25) push basket size up; tiered pricing (student/SNAP) segments willingness-to-pay.

## 2. Numbers that signal depth

**Headline scale & product**
- DashPass(+Wolt+/Deliveroo Plus) subscribers: **10M+ (Q4'21) → 15M+ (2022) → 18M+ (2023) → 22M+ (2024) → ~35M (2025)** [S5][S6].
- DoorDash US MAUs: **56M**, "over 100M annual customers," all-time high (Q4 2025) [S2]; 42M+ MAUs Dec-2024 [S6].
- Marketplace GOV **$29.7B in Q4 2025, +39% Y/Y** — and the GOV definition *includes DashPass/Wolt+/Deliveroo Plus membership fees* [S4]. (GOV = gross order value, the total dollar value of orders.)
- Q1 2026 parent revenue **$4.04B, +33% Y/Y**; EPS $1.14 [S1].

**Member behavior**
- Avg DashPass customer orders "a couple of times a week" [S2]; third-party est. ~3 orders/week ≈ ~12/month (est., not DoorDash-confirmed) [S6].
- ~**30% of US MAUs order outside restaurants**; stated goal 100% [S1][S2]. 25%+ of MAUs bought ≥1 new-vertical category by end-2024 [S6].
- DoorDash won "**1 in every 2 new customers**" trying grocery delivery for the first time [S1].

**Pricing & mechanics (US)**
- Fee: **$9.99/mo** or **$96/yr ($8/mo)**; **student 50% off (~$4.99/mo)**; **SNAP/EBT $4.99/mo** for one year [S8][S9].
- Benefit floor: **$0 delivery fee + reduced service fee on subtotals ≥ $12** (restaurants/convenience) or **≥ $25** (grocery) [S3][S9].
- Service fee: **~10% for members vs ~15–18% non-members**; avg saving **~$4–5 per eligible order**; delivery-fee saving **$2.99–$6.99** [S9].
- Extras: **5% back in DoorDash credits on pickup**, member-only deals, **5–10% off Lyft (up to 4 rides/mo)**, **Family Sharing** (1 household member free), and (2026) **Max with Ads** bundled for annual members [S7][S8].

**Competitive**
- **Uber One and DashPass** both **$9.99/mo, $96/yr** [S5][S7]; Uber One offers up to 10% off orders + 6% Uber Cash on rides — one membership across rides+eats; DashPass leans on local restaurants + Lyft + streaming perks [S7].

**Unit economics (directional)**
- DashPass orders carry **lower contribution margin per order** than non-member orders; named as a drag on US restaurant margin improvement [S2][S6]. Offset thesis: higher retention × higher frequency → more gross-profit dollars per member-year [S2]. (Exact per-order figures not disclosed.)

## 3. Wow Vault ★
*What makes an interviewer lean in on a DashPass prompt. Ranked strongest first.*

**★ DashPass is priced to lose money on the order and win on the relationship**
- **Mechanism:** DoorDash admits "DashPass orders can have lower contribution margin per order on average" [S6], and names DashPass mix as a reason US restaurant contribution margin now improves "at a lower pace" [S2]. They keep taking that hit because "subscribers retain more, they order more… produce more gross profit dollars" [S2]. (Contribution margin = what's left from an order after the costs of serving it.)
- **Why non-obvious:** most people read a subscription as a way to lift revenue-per-user or margin. Here the subscription *lowers per-order margin on purpose* to buy frequency and retention. The unit that matters isn't the order, it's the annual member cohort.
- **Deploy:** any "how would you price / measure this subscription" question — recall hook: *"They pay to make each order less profitable, because the member is the asset, not the order."*
- **Source:** [S2][S6]

**★ The fee never went up, but the value did — a "hidden price cut" that widens the moat**
- **Mechanism:** "effectively charging the same fee for DashPass" while adding grocery/retail discounted delivery, KVI (known-value-item) discounts, 5% back on pickup, Lyft discounts, and (2026) a Max-with-Ads streaming bundle for annual members [S2][S8]. Value-per-dollar rises every quarter without a headline price change.
- **Why non-obvious:** the interviewer expects "raise price to make more money." DoorDash does the opposite: hold the price, grow the bundle. The goal is lock-in through switching cost, not maxing membership revenue.
- **Deploy:** monetization / pricing-strategy prompts — recall hook: *"Same $9.99, more in the box every year."*
- **Source:** [S2][S8]

**★ Best-of-breed, not exclusive — the streaming-subscription analogy**
- **Mechanism:** management says memberships only matter "if they are best-of-breed experiences," and points to streaming: people hold several memberships and pick one per occasion [S1]. DashPass isn't trying to be your *only* food membership; it's trying to be the one you reach for on the highest-value occasions.
- **Why non-obvious:** cuts against the usual "one subscription to rule them all / bundle everything" instinct (Uber One's pitch). DoorDash accepts co-existence with Uber One and competes on winning each occasion.
- **Deploy:** competitive-strategy / positioning questions — recall hook: *"Nobody cancels Netflix to keep Hulu; you keep both."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ The real TAM ceiling is an occasions gap, not a user gap**
- **Mechanism:** "20 to 25 occasions for eating alone every single week… over 100 every single month," and DoorDash is "a tiny fraction" / "single-digit percentage of what DashPass could actually achieve" [S1][S2]. Growth isn't "sign up more people," it's "capture more of an existing member's weekly eating occasions."
- **Why non-obvious:** the metric that matters shifts from subscriber count to occasions-per-member. A mature DashPass member ordering "a couple of times a week" [S2] against a 100+/month occasion base is the headroom.
- **Deploy:** North-star / growth-lever / market-sizing prompts — recall hook: *"They're not out of users, they're out of the member's week."*
- **Source:** [S1][S2]

**★ Cross-category is the frequency amplifier — the member who buys groceries too is a different animal**
- **Mechanism:** ~30% of MAUs order outside restaurants; stated goal is 100% [S1][S2]. New-vertical growth and DashPass frequency move together — the membership makes trying grocery/DashMart easy, and each new category adds occasions to the member's week.
- **Why non-obvious:** DashPass isn't a restaurant-delivery perk anymore; it's the cross-category pass. The grocery flywheel and the membership flywheel are the same flywheel.
- **Deploy:** "how would you grow frequency" / roadmap prompts — recall hook: *"30% → 100% is a DashPass frequency story, not a grocery story."*
- **Source:** [S1][S2]

**★ Subscribers scaled 10M → 35M in four years, and 2025 was still a "record quarter" for adds**
- **Mechanism:** DashPass(+Wolt+/Deliveroo Plus) went 10M+ (2021) → 18M+ (2023) → 22M+ (2024) → ~35M (2025) [S5][S6], and Q4 2025 / Q1 2026 were *record* subscriber quarters, with growth from "both sign-ups as well as… retention" [S1][S2]. A 4-year-old base still speeding up its adds is unusual.
- **Why non-obvious:** most subscription products add fewer members as the base matures; DashPass is adding *more Y/Y* [S1].
- **Deploy:** metrics / "is this healthy" prompts — recall hook: *"35M members and still setting add records — the S-curve hasn't rolled over."*
- **Source:** [S1][S2][S5][S6]

**★ Corporate DashPass turns the consumer perk into a B2B distribution channel**
- **Mechanism:** DoorDash for Business (corporate DashPass + Meal Manager + catering + group ordering) is "off to a very, very great start," now adding workplace catering [S1]. Same membership rails, but the buyer is an employer expensing meals — higher retention, bigger baskets, a channel Uber Eats also chases.
- **Why non-obvious:** people think DashPass = consumer. The enterprise version is a quieter, stickier B2B motion on the same benefit engine.
- **Deploy:** "new growth vector / adjacent market" prompts — recall hook: *"The employer pays the $9.99 equivalent, and nobody churns a work perk."*
- **Source:** [S1]

**★ The end-to-end job — not discovery — is why the membership survives AI agents**
- **Mechanism:** DoorDash's answer to "agents will cut you out": winning the top of funnel is worthless if "it's out of stock" or the milk gets substituted — "what's the point of having the best discovery experience if we can't bring you that exact item" [S1]. The membership is loyalty to *fulfillment reliability*, which happens after checkout in the physical world where an agent can't help.
- **Why non-obvious:** connects a subscription-loyalty question to the agentic-commerce threat — the retention isn't brand, it's "they don't screw up my order."
- **Deploy:** "how does AI change this business" prompts — recall hook: *"An agent can pick the restaurant; it can't un-spoil the milk."*
- **Source:** [S1]

## 4. Reframes & mental models to borrow

- **"Best-of-breed, not exclusive."** Memberships co-exist (streaming model); compete on winning each occasion, not on being someone's only subscription. → positioning / competitive prompts. [S1]
- **"Legible value per transaction."** DashPass shows the saving *on every order* ("$0 delivery fee over $12"), not points you have to redeem later — value the member can feel each time. → loyalty-design / retention prompts. [S3][S6]
- **"Profit dollars, not margin percent."** Management won't optimize contribution-margin-per-order; it optimizes total gross-profit dollars from the cohort. → metrics / "what's the right North Star" prompts. [S2]
- **"Occasions, not users."** The ceiling is the member's 100+ monthly eating occasions, of which DoorDash owns single digits. → market-sizing / growth-lever prompts. [S1][S2]
- **"Hold the price, grow the box."** Make more by adding value inside a fixed fee (lock-in), not by raising the fee (extraction). → pricing prompts. [S2][S8]
- **"The channel partner, not the disintermediator."** Google Food Ordering / Amazon-search history: someone else can own discovery and *still* lose the customer, because retention lives in fulfillment. → AI-threat / moats prompts. [S1][S2]

## 5. Moats & Weaknesses

**Moats**
| Moat | Evidence | Deepening / eroding |
|---|---|---|
| Habit + benefit-breadth lock-in | "core driver of consumer relationship since 2017" [S2]; retention rising Y/Y [S1] | **Deepening** — each added category/perk (grocery, pickup, Lyft, Max) adds a reason to stay |
| Partner-distributed acquisition | Chase/T-Mobile/card bundles [S7][S10] | **Deepening** — banks and carriers want a delivery perk; near-zero CAC members |
| Proprietary physical-world catalog | "billions of items… cannot be scraped… we don't have to share" [S1] | **Deepening** — powers the fulfillment reliability members actually pay for |
| End-to-end fulfillment (post-checkout) | "best end-to-end beats best discovery" [S1] | **Stable/deepening** — the part agents can't replicate |
| Cross-category frequency flywheel | 30%→100% new-vertical goal; 1-in-2 new grocery customers [S1] | **Deepening** — grocery share leadership feeds member occasions |

**Weaknesses**
| Weakness | Why it bites | Where a rival attacks |
|---|---|---|
| Structurally lower per-order margin | DashPass mix drags restaurant contribution margin [S2] | A rival can cut fees without a public-company margin bar |
| Price parity with Uber One | Both $9.99/$96 — no price advantage [S5][S7] | Uber One's rides+eats bundle is a broader single-membership pitch [S7] |
| Perk-dependence / promo fatigue | Value increasingly rests on stacked extras (Lyft, Max) run by other companies | If a partner (e.g. streaming) walks, the bundle thins overnight |
| Benefit only kicks in at ≥$12/$25 | Small/solo orders don't clear the minimum → no member value on the smallest occasions [S3] | Rivals with no minimum win the sub-$12 occasion |
| Membership fee counted inside GOV | Adds subscription revenue to a headline metric [S4] | Skeptics can argue GOV growth is partly fee reclassification, not order growth |

## 6. AI impact — deflationary or moat?

| AI vector | Deflationary / commoditizing | Moat-deepening / compounding |
|---|---|---|
| Personal shopping agents (order for you) | Could commoditize discovery, turn DoorDash into a logistics API, weaken the consumer relationship the membership earns from | DoorDash bets agents "drive a small % of traffic," and history (Google Food Ordering, Amazon search) shows discovery-owners still lose retention because fulfillment happens post-checkout [S1][S2] |
| Physical-world catalog (where every banana/avocado sits) | — | Proprietary, "cannot be scraped," the data an agent needs and can't get — the reliability members pay for [S1] |
| Merchant onboarding / ops automation | — | Multi-turn models finish onboarding "with perfect quality" → more selection per member, faster [S1] |
| Ad targeting on member traffic | Ad-tech is commoditizing across the board | Applied to DoorDash's first-party member frequency data → better ROAS at "much lower ad load" [S1] |
| AI-written code (½–⅔ of code) | Cheaper feature velocity for everyone | Caveated: "shipping faster ≠ better customer outcomes… customer holds us to a higher bar" [S1] |

**Net read:** **net tailwind for DashPass.** The membership's retention rests on post-checkout fulfillment reliability, which sits on proprietary physical-world data agents can't scrape — the one asset AI *strengthens* instead of commoditizing. **The one real risk to watch:** an agent layer that owns the consumer's intent (the "where do I want dinner" moment) and pushes DoorDash down to a price-shopped fulfillment bid — turning a loyalty membership into a commodity delivery contract. DoorDash's own tell that it's watching this: it insists agents will be "partners… a choice that we'll have" [S1] — a claim it only needs to make if the downside is real.

## 7. Needs-based segments → problems → solutions
*Shreyas method — segment on the JOB the member hires DashPass to do, not demographics. Each passes the 5-Point Test (consistent needs · product-specific · targetable · prioritizable · winnable).*

**Segmentation basis:** for a delivery membership, the needs axis is **why they subscribe** — fee-avoidance math vs. time-scarcity vs. household-provisioning vs. occasion-quality vs. expensed-convenience. Demographics (age/income) cut across all five and don't predict behavior; the *job* does.

**A. The Fee-Math Optimizer** — Job (functional): make the $9.99 pay for itself and never see a delivery fee again. *Social/emotional:* feeling smart, not gouged. **Friction:** the $12/$25 minimum means small solo orders don't clear the benefit; unsure if they order enough to break even. **Nudge (extrinsic):** in-app "you've saved $X this month vs. your $9.99" running tally. **Aha:** *"third order this month and I'm already up $6."* **Today → gap:** DoorDash shows per-order savings but doesn't hammer the cumulative break-even → **gap: no persistent ROI receipt.** → Play #1.

**B. The Time-Starved Weeknight Household** — Job (functional): get dinner on the table on a chaotic weeknight without a decision or a fee. *Emotional:* remove one stressor from a hard day. **Friction:** decision fatigue ("what does everyone want"); reliability anxiety (will it be right/on time). **Nudge (intrinsic):** a reliable default — reorder the usual in two taps. **Aha:** *"it's 6:40, kids are fed, I didn't think about it."* **Today → gap:** reorder exists but isn't surfaced at the weeknight occasion → **gap: no occasion-aware default.** → Play #2.

**C. The Household Provisioner (groceries + restaurants)** — Job (functional): run the household's food supply — mid-week quick runs + weekend stock-ups — through one membership. *Personal:* be the competent one who keeps the house stocked. **Friction:** substitutions/out-of-stocks break trust; grocery delivery still "not good enough vs. walking into the store" [S1]. **Nudge (intrinsic):** trust that what's shown is in stock and arrives correct. **Aha:** *"zero substitutions, everything came."* **Today → gap:** DashMart Fulfillment Services is early / "handful of partners" [S1] → **gap: inventory accuracy not yet reliable at scale.** → Play #3.

**D. The Occasion-Splurger** — Job (social/emotional): make a specific occasion good (Friday treat, group order, game night), price-insensitive. **Friction:** wants the *exact* item/restaurant, hates substitutions or "unavailable." **Nudge (intrinsic):** the membership guarantees the good version of the occasion. **Aha:** *"got the exact thing, on time, it was the night I wanted."* **Today → gap:** DashPass under-serves the *specialness* — it's framed as savings, not occasion quality → **gap: no occasion-elevation layer.** → Play #4.

**E. The Expensed Convenience User (corporate DashPass)** — Job (functional): eat/cater on the employer's dime without an expense-report hassle. *Social:* the perk signals a good workplace. **Friction:** group catering is "exponentially difficult" (kitchen capacity, menu, logistics) [S1]; expense compliance. **Nudge (extrinsic):** employer pays; the expensing hassle is removed. **Aha:** *"lunch showed up, budget tracked, nothing to file."* **Today → gap:** DoorDash for Business is "off to a great start" but catering ops are hard [S1] → **gap: catering reliability + spend controls immature.** → Play #5.

## 8. Strategy *(Shreyas Strategy Template)*
- **One-sentence strategy:** Use a fixed-price, value-visible membership to turn occasional users into high-frequency members whose retention rests on fulfillment reliability, then grow each member across every eating and household-provisioning occasion.
- **Prioritize / Don't over-serve:** Prioritize **frequency and retention of existing members across categories** / Don't over-serve **per-order margin** (given up on purpose) or **membership-fee revenue max** (price held flat).
- **Pillars (moat → segment):** (1) Visible fee relief → Fee-Math Optimizer + Weeknight Household; (2) Cross-category pass (grocery/DashMart/pickup) → Household Provisioner; (3) Partner-distributed acquisition (banks/carriers) → all segments at low CAC; (4) Fulfillment reliability on proprietary catalog → Provisioner + Splurger + AI defense; (5) Corporate wrapper → Expensed user.
- **North star:** **member occasions captured per member per month** (against the 100+/month occasion ceiling) — not subscriber count, not per-order margin.
- **Non-priorities (trade-offs):** being anyone's *only* delivery membership (co-exist w/ Uber One); winning the sub-$12 solo occasion; raising the fee; short-term contribution margin.
- **Roadmap / metrics:**
  - **Now** — persistent savings-ROI receipt + reduce break-even anxiety. *Leading:* % of new members who hit break-even in month 1. *Lagging:* trial→paid conversion, month-1 retention.
  - **Next** — cross-category default (grocery + reorder) surfaced at the occasion. *Leading:* % members ordering ≥2 categories. *Lagging:* orders/member/month; 30%→100% new-vertical penetration.
  - **Later** — fulfillment reliability at scale (DashMart Fulfillment Services) + corporate catering. *Leading:* substitution/out-of-stock rate. *Lagging:* member retention curve slope; corporate DashPass seat count.

## 9. Contrarian bets & open tensions

- **Bet: give up per-order margin to buy the member-year.** *Bear:* if frequency/retention lift ever softens, you're left with a low-margin order mix and a public-company margin bar you can't hit — the flywheel runs backward. *Counter:* retention *and* frequency are both rising Y/Y at a mature base [S1], and profit-dollar production (not margin %) is the stated goal [S2].
- **Bet: hold the fee flat forever and make money via the bundle + ads.** *Bear:* leaves obvious revenue-per-user on the table and makes value depend on third-party perks (Lyft, Max) DoorDash doesn't control. *Counter:* lock-in and switching cost are worth more than fee extraction; ads on member traffic earn without touching the price [S1].
- **Bet: agents are channel partners, not disintermediators.** *Bear:* this time is different — a dominant personal agent could own intent and reduce DoorDash to a fulfillment bid. *Counter:* Google Food Ordering and Amazon-search both owned discovery and still lost retention, because the physical-world last mile is where trust is won or lost [S1][S2].
- **Best skeptic angle:** membership fees are counted *inside* Marketplace GOV [S4] — so part of the celebrated GOV growth is subscription revenue reclassification, and DashPass's per-order margin drag means order-level economics are weaker than the top line implies.
- **Valuation tension:** the whole DASH multiple leans on frequency/retention compounding; DashPass is the mechanism, but its margin drag and price parity with Uber One mean the moat is *behavioral* (habit), not *structural* (price/tech) — fragile if a rival out-bundles it.

## 10. Big Picture — Looks Wrong, Is Right

**A. Wise refusals**
- **Not raising the $9.99 fee despite adding grocery/pickup/Lyft/streaming value** → the restraint is right because the goal is switching-cost lock-in and occasion-capture, not membership revenue-per-user; a price hike would spike churn and hand price-sensitive members to Uber One right when DoorDash is trying to deepen frequency. [S2][S8]
- **Not chasing per-order contribution margin on DashPass orders** → right because the member cohort's lifetime gross-profit dollars dwarf any single order's margin; optimizing the order would starve the frequency it's meant to buy. [S2]
- **Not forcing DashPass to be your only delivery membership** → right; the streaming analogy says exclusivity is unwinnable and unnecessary — win per-occasion share, don't demand monogamy. [S1]
- **Keeping ad load low even as Ads hits record revenue** → right because members are the frequency engine; spamming them to juice ad revenue would erode the daily-use habit the whole model rests on. [S1]

**B. Counterintuitive moves**
- **Bundling a streaming service (Max with Ads) into a food-delivery membership** → looks like scope-creep marketing; actually a cheap switching-cost multiplier — one more reason not to cancel that has nothing to do with food. [S8]
- **Giving the membership away free through banks and carriers** → looks like giving away the product; actually acquires the highest-LTV, payment-attached members at near-zero CAC and sets up a card relationship. [S7][S10]
- **Counting membership fees inside GOV** → looks like metric inflation; actually reflects the real strategy — the membership and the marketplace are one economic system, not two, so measuring them together is honest to how the flywheel works. [S4]
- **Pushing 30% of members toward 100% cross-category** → looks like a distraction from the profitable restaurant core; actually the only way to grow a mature member's frequency is to add categories to their week. [S1][S2]

DashMart Fulfillment Services is live with only "a handful of partners" [S1]. → *opportunity:* substitutions are the #1 trust-killer for the Household Provisioner (§7C); getting inventory accuracy right converts the highest-frequency, highest-basket member segment. → Play #3. [S1]
- **No persistent, cumulative savings-ROI receipt (my judgment).** → *why:* DoorDash shows per-order savings and a green badge but doesn't durably surface "you've saved $X vs. your $9.99 this year" — the break-even anxiety that makes the Fee-Math Optimizer cancel goes unaddressed. → *opportunity:* a running savings ledger is a cheap retention/renewal lever right at the cancel moment. → Play #1. (my judgment; not in sources)
- **Sub-$12 solo occasions are unserved (my judgment).** → *why:* the $12 minimum for member benefits means the smallest, most frequent solo occasions (a coffee, an $8 lunch) give no membership value → the member learns DashPass "doesn't help" for small orders and habit doesn't form there. → *opportunity:* a small-order member benefit (capped credit, waived minimum on Nth order) captures the highest-frequency occasion band. → Play in §12. ## 12. What's missing → Plays to run *(10× / 100× — hypotheses, not today)*

- **Play #1 — Savings Ledger + auto-renew defense.** Persistent "you've saved $X vs. $9.99" receipt, shown pre-renewal. *Closes:* gap (a), serves Fee-Math Optimizer. *Why DashPass:* it already computes per-order savings — just make them cumulative and durable. *10×* on renewal retention at zero delivery cost. *Proof:* A/B the ledger on the renewal screen, measure churn delta. [S3][S6]

[S1][S2]

- **[Product sense]** *"How would you grow DashPass?"* → Reframe from subscriber count to **occasions-per-member** (§1, §8 North Star); the lever is cross-category frequency (30%→100%) + occasion-aware defaults (Play #2), not more signups. [S1][S2]

- **[Estimation]** *"Size DashPass's frequency headroom."* → 35M members × (100+ monthly eating occasions − ~12 current DoorDash orders) ≈ the occasions gap (est.); DoorDash owns single-digit % today. §1, §3. [S1][S2]
